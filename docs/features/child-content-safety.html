Content safety for child-facing generation

How AI-generated content reaches a child only through a gate that fails closed, cannot be forgotten, and cannot be talked past — and what the humans around the child can and cannot do. Implements Spec 096 on the guardian machinery of Spec 095. Back to the index.

The stance: why "mostly safe" is not a target

Most platform features balance strictness against convenience. This one does not, because the failure modes are asymmetric: the cost of over-blocking is a child told "that one didn't work, try again"; the cost of under-blocking is a six-year-old seeing something no setting can unsee. Every tie in this design breaks the same way — fail closed. A classifier that is down, slow, or erroring means the content is not delivered, full stop, and a classifier outage is a paging incident, because a safety check silently degrading to "safe" is the worst defect this system can have.

Where safety lives — and the boundary it refuses to cross

Safety is a property of generation, not of storage. The gate runs while content is still a request and a response — the only moment the prompt, the model, the child's age band and the output are all in hand at once. By the time bytes reach the workspace layer, they have already been judged, so the storage layer's deliberate ignorance of file contents survives intact.

A real limit, stated on the contract itself The corollary: a file arriving by import or sync is not inspected, by anyone. We are responsible for what we generate. A product that quietly began scanning everything a family stores would be a different and much worse product — this line is drawn in the IContentSafetyGate documentation precisely so nobody "fixes" it later by accident.

Defence in depth: six layers, each honest about its limits

No single layer is trusted. Each row below says what the layer does and what it cannot do — the second column is the design input, because a layer's gap is what the next layer exists for.

LayerDoesCannot
L1 — structural

L2 — input

L4 — output

L5 — human

L6 — after

L3 is necessary and nowhere near sufficient Provider safety settings are built for general audiences. "Safe for a general audience" and "safe for a six-year-old" are different thresholds, and the gap between them contains most of what actually goes wrong here — the frightening, the violent-but-not-graphic, the age-inappropriately intense. Provider settings also change without notice and differ between vendors. The rule: turn every provider setting to its strictest value, then design as though it were not there. The test suite includes a case that passes a provider's strictest setting and still fails L4.
The strongest lever is structural Narrowing what can be generated beats filtering what was. A filter is an adversarial contest against a model's entire output distribution, run in milliseconds, on every request, forever. A constrained request space is a design decision made once. Curated characters alone remove the entire real-person-likeness category — and most of the frightening-figure category — at a stroke.

Age bands: computed from a date, stored on the party, never supplied by a caller

Bands change three things: what may be asked (L1), how strict the classifiers are (L2/L4), and whether an adult is in the path (L5).

Under 6Curated only. No free text.Strictest. Mild peril blocked.On by default.

Three decisions behind that table, each of which closed a real hole:

  • A birth year cannot schedule a band change. Advance bands on 1 January and a December-born five-year-old gets a ten-year-old's prompts and thresholds almost a year early. Spec 095 already collects an exact birth date transiently at enrolment, so the band boundary dates are computed then and persisted on the party (SafetyBand, SafetyBandChangesOn) — and, because a set of boundary dates recovers the birth date, they are classified and protected as birth-date-equivalent data. A scheduled job advances bands on the day, not the year.
  • Safety banding tracks minority, not consent capacity. The party carries two separate bands — ConsentBand (who may consent: below-threshold / self-consenting / adult) and SafetyBand — because a UK 13-year-old starts consenting for their own data and remains a protected minor until 18. Deriving one from the other left ages 13–17 matching no safety row at all.
  • Unknown age is the strictest band, never the loosest. A party with no attested age is treated as under 6. The cost of over-strictness is a support conversation; the other direction is an incident.
The band is not a request field SafetyRequest does not have a band property at all. A request that carried its own band could claim adult for a six-year-old and skip every threshold and every guardian hold with one value. The gate resolves the band itself, through ISafetyBandReader, from the party record the age-transition job maintains. What a caller cannot say, a caller cannot lie about.

The humans: why Arke's own accept gate does not transfer

Arke Studio's core mechanic — nothing enters the record without a human accept — is reused twice in this platform (sync divergence, the tiered mutation gate). The obvious move was to reuse it a third time as the safety control. It does not work here: in Studio, the human reviewing a proposal is the author protecting their own record; in Kids, the recipient is the person being protected. Putting the child in the review seat means showing them the thing they were supposed to be shielded from and asking them to judge it. The accept gate cannot be the safety control when the accepting human is the one at risk.

So the human layer is the guardian, deliberately optional and secondary:

  • Pre-review — content held until an adult approves. Default on for under-6, available to any parent (GuardianPreReviewService; a held item is HeldForReview, which is a state, not a refusal).
  • Visibility — an adult can always see what has been generated for their child, pre-review or not. A Children's Code parental-controls expectation, and simply the right default.
  • Never the primary control — a parent will approve in bulk within a week; the automated layers must stand alone.
  • Transparent to the child — where an adult reviews or can see their work, the child is told, in age-appropriate terms. Covert monitoring is explicitly out, whatever a parent asks for.

What happens on a detection

Three cases, kept strictly apart, because conflating them produces a product that is either unsafe or unusable:

CaseThe child seesOperationallyBilling
Blocked — judged unsafe A plain, unalarming message: this one didn't work, try again. Never the content, a category name, or a red warning — a seven-year-old told they triggered a "violence filter" learns they did something wrong; they did not. Incident recorded; guardian can see it; nobody is paged for an ordinary block. Not billed. The gate itself releases the Spec 087 meter reservation on any outcome other than Allowed — charging a family a story credit for content we refused to show is indefensible, and leaving the release to caller discipline means it is wrong on exactly the paths nobody tested.
Check unavailable — classifier down/timed out The same plain message. A failure to check is not the child's problem to understand. An outage. Pages. The verdict carries WasUnavailable so monitoring cannot confuse it with a block.
Modality disabled — e.g. video switched off The feature simply is not offered. Not an outage, must not page. A feature nobody enabled cannot be "down"; conflating the two pages an operator forever for video that was never turned on. Nothing was reserved.

One structural detail with an audit consequence: an L2 input block happens before dispatch, so no generation ever ran — and the decision record's generation reference is therefore nullable. Requiring it would force the implementation to fabricate an AiRun for an AI execution that never happened, corrupting the platform's "every AI action is auditable" rule from inside the safety system. A test pins that no fabricated run is ever written.

False positives, and the boundary on guardian appeal

The failure mode nobody plans for A knight fighting a dragon is the single most common request a six-year-old makes. A classifier strict enough to block genuinely violent output will also block a great deal of ordinary children's storytelling — fairy tales are full of wolves, witches, and characters in real danger. A product that refuses half of them is broken in a quieter way than the alternative, but far more often — and it teaches children the product does not work. Before refusing outright, the system retries with tightened structural constraints; a constrained re-generation usually produces an acceptable version of the same idea, which is a far better outcome for the child than a refusal.

Guardian appeal exists for exactly this — and an earlier draft made it an unconditional release, which would have let any guardian deliver anything to a child by clicking through the last line of defence. The boundary now has two tiers:

CategoryAppeal
Non-overridable — sexual content, explicit self-harm instruction, and the preservation category below No release, by anyone, ever. Not viewable by the guardian either. An appeal here is a signal, not a request: recorded, and repeated appeals against this category on one account escalate to a person. A guardian account is not proof of good intent, and the category where that matters most is precisely this one.
Reviewable — violence, peril, frightening imagery, everything age-banded The guardian may view and release to their own child only. This is where false positives actually live, and where a parent's judgement should outrank a threshold.
A release is a decision, not ground truth A release records "this guardian chose to allow it", which is a different fact from "the classifier was wrong". Feeding releases back into threshold tuning as false-positive labels would teach the system to pass content reviewers still consider unsafe, one permissive parent at a time, with the drift showing up as an improving metric. False positives are assigned only by independent review; releases direct attention to a category, never move a threshold automatically. And false-negative rates are not pretended into existence — you cannot count what you did not detect — so the honest instrument is a scheduled evaluation: a held-out corpus per band, run against live configuration, labelled by named reviewers, plus consented sampling of delivered output.

Enforcement is a type: the permit

Everything above is policy. What makes it unbypassable is the same move the platform used for mutating AI tools (Spec 032's build-time gate): make the unsafe path fail to compile, not fail review.

SafetyVerdict = ScreenOutputAsync(request, content) │ ├─ Allowed ────────► carries a ContentDeliveryPermit └─ anything else ──► carries null ContentDeliveryPermit • constructor INTERNAL to the safety assembly — no caller can new one up • names the DecisionId → every delivery traceable to a recorded verdict • names the MODALITY → a speech permit does not authorise an image • names the EXACT content reference • Authorises(modality, reference) — the check delivery paths run delivery paths take a PERMIT, not a boolean → "did you remember to check?" is not a convention, it is the type system → there is nothing to deliver WITH if you skipped the gate

The permit names the artefact deliberately. A permit carrying only the subject would be a bearer token: hold any valid one and pair it with a different file — or pair an image permit with audio — and unclassified content is laundered through a delivery type that looks checked. (The same "a name is not an authorisation" reasoning as the sync protocol's hash rule.) An architecture test suite (ContentSafetyBoundaryTests) additionally pins that child-facing generation paths cannot be constructed around the gate.

Classifier calls themselves route through AiRoutePolicy like every other model call — safety is not allowed a private routing mechanism — with one extra bound: Spec 095 requires the consent text to name the external companies a child's content is sent to, so routing for child content is constrained to the consented vendor list. An automatic failover to a vendor the family has never heard of would be a consent breach delivered by a reliability feature, which is precisely how it would otherwise happen in production.

When the child's own words are the concern

Children disclose things in stories. Both instinctive responses are wrong: ignoring it (out of scope), and building automated risk-detection over children's creative writing (feels responsible, is not). An automated flag will be wrong very often — children write about scary things because children write about scary things — and the consequences of wrong land on a real family. The position is deliberately narrow:

  • No risk-detection classifiers over creative writing. We are a storytelling product, not a safeguarding service, and must not act like one.
  • Explicit self-harm content is blocked as output — a content rule about what a model may generate, not a judgement about the child.
  • Gentle signposting where input explicitly concerns self-harm: a non-clinical pointer to help, written with professional input. No diagnosis, no alarm, no accusation.
  • Guardian visibility is the honest mechanism — the adults who can actually help already see what their child creates.
  • A written route for staff who encounter something concerning through support work: a named responsible person, not an ad-hoc decision. Policy, not automation.

The one absolute exception: preservation and reporting

If child sexual abuse material is ever generated, uploaded or detected, there are legal obligations to preserve and report — not discretionary, not delete-and-forget, not something engineering decides in the moment. The engineering behaviour is to make the path exist before it is needed:

  • Detection at this category escalates to a person immediately — never a classifier's judgement alone.
  • Preservation is automatic on detection and overrides ordinary retention and deletion requests — including a subject-access deletion, which cannot be used to destroy evidence.
  • Access to preserved material is restricted to named individuals; every access attempt is logged (IPreservedMaterialService); escalations stay queryable until acknowledged.
  • The retention sweeper explicitly skips anything under a preservation flag and logs the skip — so preservation and deletion cannot silently contend.
  • A written procedure with a named responsible person and counsel engaged exists before launch, per jurisdiction.

Evidence, and the tension in keeping it

Every verdict is recorded — including allowed ones — because a delivery later identified as a false negative must be reconstructible, and an audit trail covering only blocks covers everything except the failure it exists for. Each record carries the AiRun ids of the generation and every classifier call, tying the safety trail into the platform's standing audit spine. The counter-tension — this is children's content — is handled by retention as a mechanism, not a policy statement: a Quartz retention sweeper shipped with the first incident record (artefacts accumulate the moment blocking works), running per tenant, with its own failure treated as a retention incident. Thresholds per band and category live in versioned data (AnkSafetyPolicies), so tuning needs no deployment and an old incident is reviewable against the thresholds in force at the time.
