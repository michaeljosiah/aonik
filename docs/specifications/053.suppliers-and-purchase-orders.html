Spec 053 Status: Draft Size: Medium Priority: P3 Domain: Commerce / Sourcing / Ordering

Suppliers, supplier catalog & purchase orders — the reorder moment

Adds the procurement slice of the maker-operations family: a Supplier master, a supplier catalog (who sells which ingredient, at what pack and price), and the ability for an operator to place a purchase order — to "order more" of a raw material when stock runs low. Crucially, a purchase order is not a new entity: it is a new OrderType on the shared Order spine (ADR 011), reusing Order/OrderItem with ingredient soft-ref lines and an inverted direction of money — we are the payer, the supplier is the payee. This spec builds on Spec 050 (the maker-operations keystone: the Ingredient master), and consumes Spec 051 default cost and Spec 052 low-stock alerts. Everything lives in Aonik.Commerce and references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering — never Aonik.Finance: the PO rides the spine, not Finance.

1. Summary

The operations manager's day (Spec 050 §2) hits a reorder moment: a low-stock alert fires ("rice below reorder point"), and they need to order more from a supplier. Two things are missing to make that possible: a record of who we buy from and at what price, and a record of the order we placed. This spec adds both.

It introduces two new anemic entities in Aonik.Commerce for supplier master data, and — the architecturally interesting move — models the purchase order itself as a new OrderType on the existing shared Order spine rather than a standalone Commerce entity:

  • Supplier — a counterparty we buy raw materials from (name, currency, lead time, payment terms), optionally soft-linked to a platform Party.
  • SupplierIngredient — the supplier catalog / price list: this supplier sells this ingredient at this PackSize (a 25 kg sack) for this PackPrice — the buy-side unit conversion from purchase pack to stock base unit.
  • A purchase order = an Order whose OrderType is the new value "PurchaseOrder", with a Supplier party role, created via the spine's IOrderService.CreateAsync. Its lines reuse OrderItem with the ingredient as a soft reference. Because OrderType is an open string, this needs no schema change to the Order tables — only the two supplier tables are new.
Success signals An operator (or the commerce-agent) can register a supplier, add supplier-ingredient price-list rows, and place a purchase order — either from explicit lines or seeded from a Spec 052 LowStockAlert (defaulting price/pack from SupplierIngredient and cost from Spec 051) — then move it Draft → Submitted. The PO is a real Order on the spine, visible to the ordering layer, with lines that soft-reference ingredients. dotnet build Aonik.sln stays green with Aonik.Commerce referencing only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering (no Finance); an architecture test still proves the boundary; dotnet test Aonik.sln stays green with new unit tests over supplier CRUD, PO creation on the spine, and the shortfall-seed path.

2. Context & motivation

Spec 052 gives the operator visibility that a raw material is running out (a LowStockAlert). The natural next action — "order more" — has nowhere to land today. There is no supplier to order from, no price to order at, and no order record to track from placement through receipt. This spec closes that gap for the create + lifecycle of a purchase order. Two adjacent concerns are deliberately excluded:

  • Receiving the goods (stock-up + cost refresh when the sacks arrive) is Spec 054.
  • Paying the supplier (a PaymentIntent to the supplier's account) is a funding follow-up — high-tier, Finance-mediated — and is out of scope here (§10, §21).

The interesting design tension is where a purchase order lives. A PO is, structurally, exactly what the Order spine was generalised to hold: a record of an intent to transact — who the parties are, what amounts and currencies, what line items, and a lifecycle. A customer ProductPurchase already rides that spine. A purchase order is the same shape with the direction of money reversed (we pay outward instead of collecting inward). Rather than duplicate the spine inside Commerce, this spec treats a PO as one more OrderType on the shared spine — the cleanest expression of ADR 011's "Order is a core concept, not Finance-private."

Scope discipline (family rule): build the thin procurement slice this persona needs — a supplier list, a price list, and a PO you can place and submit. Not a general procurement suite: multi-supplier price comparison, preferred-supplier logic, blanket/standing orders, RFQs, approvals routing, and three-way match are noted as Open, not built.

5. Scope (in / out)

In scope

  • Supplier entity + ISupplierService (create / update / list / deactivate) + admin endpoints.
  • SupplierIngredient entity + upsert (per supplier × ingredient) + admin endpoint — the supplier price list.
  • A purchase order created as a new OrderType ("PurchaseOrder") on the Order spine, with a Supplier party role and OrderItem lines whose ProductId soft-refs an ingredient; created via IOrderService.CreateAsync.
  • IPurchaseOrderService: create from explicit lines or seeded from a Spec 052 LowStockAlert shortfall (default pack/price from SupplierIngredient, default cost from Spec 051); submit; lifecycle Draft → Submitted → Received → Closed/Cancelled via IOrderService.TransitionAsync.
  • Admin endpoints: create supplier, upsert supplier-ingredient, create PO, submit PO, list POs.
  • The two new Commerce DbSets on CommerceDbContext + AonikDbContext; one tool-generated migration (CommerceSuppliersAndPurchaseOrders).
  • Commerce-agent read tool (list suppliers) + medium-gated write tools (create supplier, create PO, submit PO).

Out of scope

  • Receiving goods — stock-up + actual-cost refresh + PO→Received on arrival — is Spec 054. This spec only advances a PO up to Submitted; the Received/Closed transitions are defined here but driven by 054.
  • Paying the supplier — a PaymentIntent to the supplier account, the outward-money leg — is a funding follow-up (commerce_pay_purchase_order, high-tier Finance Proposal); noted, not built (§10, §21).
  • Multi-supplier price comparison, preferred-supplier selection, blanket/standing orders, RFQs, PO approval routing, three-way match, supplier onboarding/KYC — Open, not built.
  • A unit-of-measure conversion engine. SupplierIngredient.PackSize is a single per-row buy-side factor (25 kg → kg), not a general conversion table.

6. Constraints & guardrails

  • No reference to Finance. Aonik.Commerce references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering. The PO rides the spine (in Aonik.Ordering), not Finance; supplier payment is deferred precisely to keep this boundary (ADR-005).
  • Reuse the spine; add no new order concept. A purchase order is an Order, not a parallel PurchaseOrder entity (ADR-011). OrderType is an open string, so "PurchaseOrder" is additive — no Order-table schema change (§10, §14).
  • Anemic entities. Supplier/SupplierIngredient are { get; set; } data containers implementing ITenantScoped + AuditableEntity (ADR-002); all logic lives in services returning DTOs.
  • Single migration stream. EF-CLI-generated against AonikDbContext; Ank-prefixed tables (AnkSuppliers, AnkSupplierIngredients); CommerceDbContext is runtime scoping with no migrations; agents never hand-author migrations.
  • Direction of money is explicit. On a PO we are the Payer; the supplier is the payee (Supplier party role). This is the inverse of a customer ProductPurchase and must be modelled, not implied (§11).
  • Agents propose; systems execute. Supplier list is a direct read tool; create-supplier / create-PO / submit-PO are medium (in-session confirmation, args-hash-bound); every AI action records an AiRun. Supplier payment would be high-tier (Finance Proposal) — not built.

8. Domain model

Two new anemic entities, both ITenantScoped, both inheriting AuditableEntity. The purchase order itself has no new entity — it is an Order + OrderItems on the spine (§10). 

class Supplier           { Guid Id, TenantId;
                           Guid? PartyId;      // soft ref to a counterparty Party (no FK); null if unlinked
                           string  Name;
                           string  Currency;   // the currency we buy from this supplier in
                           int?    LeadTimeDays;
                           string? PaymentTerms;   // free text, e.g. "Net 30"
                           bool    IsActive; }

class SupplierIngredient { Guid Id, TenantId, SupplierId, IngredientId;   // the supplier catalog / price list
                           string?  Sku;        // the supplier's SKU for this ingredient
                           decimal  PackSize;   // buy-side unit: qty of ingredient BaseUnit per pack (a 25kg sack)
                           decimal  PackPrice;  // price for one pack
                           string   Currency;
                           int?     LeadTimeDays; }   // overrides Supplier.LeadTimeDays for this line

Supplier.PartyId is a soft reference (opaque Guid?, no FK) to a platform counterparty Party — mirroring how Order.PayerPartyId soft-refs a Party across the module boundary. A supplier can exist unlinked; linking is a later enrichment, not a prerequisite (§9, §20). SupplierIngredient is keyed by supplier × ingredient and carries the buy-side conversion: PackSize says how many of the ingredient's BaseUnit one purchasable pack contains (buy a 25 kg sack, stock in kg), so PackPrice / PackSize is the per-base-unit cost.

9. Supplier & supplier catalog

A Supplier is a counterparty we buy raw materials from — the sourcing-side mirror of a customer. It is intentionally light: a name, the currency we transact in, an optional default lead time and payment terms, an active flag. It is not a full party/KYC record; where a real platform Party exists (e.g. the supplier is also an onboarded counterparty), PartyId soft-links to it, but the supplier row stands alone otherwise. This keeps Commerce free of a Platform/party ProjectReference — the soft-ref is the same discipline the spine already uses for PayerPartyId.

SupplierIngredient is the supplier catalog / price list: one row per (supplier, ingredient) the supplier sells us, carrying the supplier's Sku, the pack we buy in (PackSize in the ingredient's BaseUnit), the PackPrice, and an optional per-line lead time. This is where the buy-side unit conversion promised in Spec 050 §10 lands: the kitchen meters rice in kg (the base unit), but the supplier sells 25 kg sacks — PackSize = 25 bridges the two. There is no general UoM engine; each row is a single, explicit factor. A given ingredient may have several supplier rows across different suppliers; choosing between them (cheapest, preferred) is Open — v1 leaves supplier choice to the operator.

10. PurchaseOrder as an OrderType on the spine

This is the key decision of the spec. A purchase order is not a new Commerce entity — it is an Order on the shared Ordering spine whose OrderType carries the new known value "PurchaseOrder". Because ADR 011 made OrderType an open string (the enum/OrderTypeCodes helper is a known-values convenience, so new types are additive), adding "PurchaseOrder" requires no schema change to AnkOrders/AnkOrderItems. Likewise OrderPartyRoles gains a "Supplier" constant — a string value, not a schema change. Only the two supplier tables are new (§14).

A PO's lines reuse the retail line shape already on OrderItem (the same columns a ProductPurchase uses), with one documented reinterpretation: ProductId, an opaque Guid? soft reference with no FK, holds the IngredientId instead of a product id. The spine treats ProductId as opaque across the module boundary, so this reuse is safe and requires nothing new — but it is a reinterpretation, so a DetailsJson discriminator makes the intent unambiguous and the FX/remittance fields simply go unused:

PurchaseOrder line → OrderItem column meaning ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── ItemType → "PurchaseOrder" the line kind on the spine Quantity → Quantity number of packs (or base-unit qty) ordered UnitPrice → UnitPrice supplier unit cost (per pack / per base unit) line total → AmountIn Quantity × UnitPrice (existing money column) ingredient (soft) → ProductId IngredientId — opaque Guid soft-ref, NO FK supplier SKU → Sku SupplierIngredient.Sku discriminator+extra → DetailsJson {"kind":"purchase-order-line","ingredientId":…,"unit":…} (unused on a PO) → AmountOut, CurrencyOut, FxQuoteId, … left unset — FX/remittance fields

The PO is created by calling the spine directly — IOrderService.CreateAsync with OrderType = "PurchaseOrder", the Supplier and Payer party roles, and the ingredient lines above. There is no second "order" concept in Commerce to keep in sync. Billing (if the supplier invoices) still flows through Invoice (Invoice.OrderId) and funding through a PaymentIntent, exactly as the financial order types do — but both of those are the deferred funding follow-up, not part of this spec.

Why not a standalone entity A parallel Commerce.PurchaseOrder would duplicate the spine's parties, lines, amounts, currencies, and lifecycle — and immediately drift from it. Riding the spine gives POs the ordering layer's history, status machine, and party model for free, and keeps ADR 011's promise that Order is a core concept, not Finance-private. The tradeoff (overloading retail OrderItem columns for ingredient lines) is managed by the mapping above plus the DetailsJson discriminator (§18, §20).

11. Direction of money — we are the payer

A customer ProductPurchase collects money inward: the customer is the Payer, the tenant/merchant is the receiver. A purchase order is the inverse: money flows outward from us to the supplier. This direction is modelled explicitly on the order's party roles, not left implicit in the order type:

Role on the orderProductPurchase (inbound)PurchaseOrder (outbound)
PayerThe customerUs (the tenant) — we pay
CounterpartyMerchant / seller (Receiver/Payee)The Supplier — receives payment
Money movesInward (collect)Outward (pay)

So a PO carries a Payer role for the tenant and a new "Supplier" OrderPartyRole for the counterparty. Getting this direction right is what makes the eventual funding leg (a PaymentIntent paying the supplier) unambiguous — and mismodelling it (treating a PO like an inbound sale) is the risk the explicit Supplier role is there to prevent (§18). The outward payment itself is the deferred high-tier follow-up (§21); this spec only records the intent and the direction, never moves money.

12. Creating a PO from a low-stock shortfall (Spec 052)

The reorder moment starts from a Spec 052 LowStockAlert. IPurchaseOrderService can seed a draft PO directly from an alert so the operator does not re-key it:

IPurchaseOrderService.CreateFromShortfallAsync(alertId, supplierId) → load the LowStockAlert (IngredientId, ReorderPoint, AvailableAtRaise) — Spec 052 → find the SupplierIngredient row for (supplierId, IngredientId) — this spec default UnitPrice ← SupplierIngredient.PackPrice default packs ← ceil( shortfall / SupplierIngredient.PackSize ) // shortfall in base units fallback unit cost← active IngredientCost.UnitCost — Spec 051 (if no catalog row) → build one OrderItem line (ProductId = IngredientId, Sku, AmountIn = qty × price, DetailsJson) → IOrderService.CreateAsync( OrderType = "PurchaseOrder", Supplier + Payer roles, [line] ) → Draft PO → (Spec 052) the alert may move Open → Ordered once a PO references it

Prices/packs default from the supplier catalog (SupplierIngredient); when no catalog row exists, the per-base-unit cost falls back to the Spec 051 IngredientCost so a PO can still be drafted. The operator can also create a PO from explicit lines (no alert) — the seed path is a convenience over the same CreateAsync, not a separate order concept. Flipping the source LowStockAlert to Ordered is a Spec 052 status concern; this spec supplies the PO it points at.

13. PO lifecycle

A purchase order moves through the spine's status machine via IOrderService.TransitionAsync — the same mechanism every order type uses. This spec drives it up to Submitted; the later transitions are defined here but effected by goods receipt (Spec 054):

Draft ──submit──▶ Submitted ──(Spec 054 receipt)──▶ Received ──▶ Closed │ │ └──── Cancelled ◀───┘ (either state may be cancelled before receipt)
StateMeaningDriven by
DraftCreated (explicit or shortfall-seeded); editable; nothing committed.this spec (CreateAsync)
SubmittedPlaced with the supplier; awaiting delivery.this spec (SubmitAsync → TransitionAsync)
ReceivedGoods arrived; stock & cost updated.Spec 054
ClosedFully received / finalised.Spec 054
CancelledAbandoned before receipt.this spec

IPurchaseOrderService.SubmitAsync validates the draft (a supplier, at least one line, positive quantities) and calls TransitionAsync Draft → Submitted. Funding is not a state in this machine — a supplier is paid by a separate PaymentIntent leg (the deferred follow-up), which can happen against a Submitted or Received PO without adding a lifecycle state here.

14. Agent & tools

The existing commerce-agent gains supplier/purchase-order tools, classified by CommerceToolApprovalManifest and wrapped by the Spec 032 gate — no change to the gate:

ToolTierEffect
commerce_list_suppliersreadDirect execution; lists suppliers (and, by argument, their catalog rows) in tenant scope.
commerce_create_suppliermediumRegister a supplier; in-session confirmation, args-hash-bound; records an AiRun.
commerce_create_purchase_ordermediumPlace a PO on the spine (explicit lines or from a LowStockAlert); medium-gated; records an AiRun.
commerce_submit_purchase_ordermediumDraft → Submitted via the spine; medium-gated; records an AiRun.
commerce_pay_purchase_orderhighFollow-up, not built. Funds a PO via a PaymentIntent to the supplier — money movement, so a durable Finance Proposal, never in-band (§21).

Every write tool is medium — an everyday domain write that runs in-band once, after a human confirms, bound to the arguments hash. Placing and submitting a PO records intent and lifecycle only; it never moves money, which is why it stops at medium. The only high-tier action in this area — paying the supplier — is the deferred follow-up and is explicitly out of scope.

15. CommerceDbContext & migrations

Two new Ank-prefixed tables — AnkSuppliers and AnkSupplierIngredients — are created by one EF-CLI migration against AonikDbContext. Each entity is added to both CommerceDbContext (runtime scoping) and AonikDbContext (canonical stream); both are tenant-scoped (neither global). Configurations live in Persistence/Configurations/Sourcing as IEntityTypeConfiguration<T> classes.

The purchase order itself needs no schema change: it reuses the existing AnkOrders and AnkOrderItems tables. "PurchaseOrder" is a new OrderType value and "Supplier" a new OrderPartyRole value — both are string values in columns that already exist (OrderType is an open string), so the migration touches only the two supplier tables. 

dotnet ef migrations add CommerceSuppliersAndPurchaseOrders --project src/Aonik.Infrastructure --startup-project src/Aonik.Api

16. Phased rollout

Phase 1 — Supplier & supplier catalog

Who we buy from, and at what pack and price.
  • Supplier + SupplierIngredient entities + configs + DbSets on CommerceDbContext and AonikDbContext; ISupplierService (supplier create/update/list/deactivate, supplier-ingredient upsert) returning DTOs; admin endpoints; the CommerceSuppliersAndPurchaseOrders migration (creates AnkSuppliers, AnkSupplierIngredients).

  • OrderTypeCodes gains "PurchaseOrder"; OrderPartyRoles gains "Supplier"; IPurchaseOrderService (create from explicit lines or shortfall-seed, submit) built over IOrderService.CreateAsync/TransitionAsync; ingredient soft-ref line mapping (§10) with DetailsJson discriminator; admin endpoints (create / submit / list POs); unit tests for line mapping, direction-of-money party roles, and the shortfall seed.

Phase 3 — Commerce-agent tools

The agentic surface.
  • Read tool (list suppliers) + medium-gated write tools (create supplier, create PO, submit PO) added to CommerceAgentTools and classified in CommerceToolApprovalManifest; routed through IToolApprovalGate.GateAll (fails closed). The high-tier commerce_pay_purchase_order is documented as a follow-up, not registered.

21. Decisions baked in

  • PurchaseOrder is an OrderType on the shared spine (per ADR-011), not a standalone Commerce entity — reuse the spine's parties, lines, amounts, and lifecycle; add no new order concept.
  • Additive by open string. OrderTypeCodes gains "PurchaseOrder" and OrderPartyRoles gains "Supplier" as string values — no Order-table schema change.
  • Line reuse. PO lines reuse OrderItem; ProductId (opaque soft-ref) carries IngredientId; AmountIn holds the line total; a DetailsJson discriminator marks the kind.
  • Supplier is a soft PartyId, not a standalone party — a light sourcing-side counterparty; link to a platform Party when one exists, stand alone otherwise (no Platform ProjectReference).
  • Direction of money is explicit: we are the Payer, the supplier is the payee — the inverse of a customer ProductPurchase.
  • Funding deferred; no Finance reference. This spec covers create + lifecycle only; paying the supplier is a high-tier Finance Proposal follow-up (commerce_pay_purchase_order), and receiving goods is Spec 054.
  • Grow in place in Aonik.Commerce (no new module); references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering; migrations stay with AonikDbContext.

22. Open design decisions

Open PO-as-OrderType vs. a standalone Commerce PurchaseOrder entity. A dedicated entity would keep procurement fields (expected-delivery, supplier-ack, receiving progress) close and avoid reinterpreting retail OrderItem columns; riding the spine reuses its parties/lines/lifecycle and honours ADR 011. Recommendation: ride the spine (an OrderType), managing the column-reuse tradeoff with the §10 mapping and DetailsJson discriminator; revisit only if procurement-specific header fields multiply.
Open Funding a PO via PaymentIntent to the supplier. The outward-money leg (commerce_pay_purchase_order) — high-tier, a durable Finance Proposal, executed by the Spec 030 dispatcher. Deferred; this spec deliberately keeps Commerce free of Finance and records only the order intent and direction.
Open Multi-supplier price comparison / preferred supplier. When several SupplierIngredient rows exist for one ingredient, auto-selecting the cheapest or a flagged preferred supplier. v1 leaves supplier choice to the operator at PO time.
Open Blanket / standing orders. A recurring or open-quantity PO drawn down over time (call-offs) rather than a one-shot order. Not in v1; each PO is a discrete order on the spine.
