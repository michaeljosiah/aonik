Spec 050 Status: Draft Size: Medium Priority: P2 Domain: Commerce / Production

Ingredient master & Recipe / Bill-of-Materials — the maker-operations keystone

Adds the two foundational master-data concepts a maker business needs on top of the retail storefront that Spec 042 already delivered: a raw-material / ingredient master and a recipe (a bill of materials) that says which ingredients — and how much — a finished product is made from. This is the keystone of the maker-operations spec family (050–057): ingredient costing, raw-material stock, procurement, production planning, kitchen sheets, and margin reporting all hang off the recipe defined here. Everything lives in Aonik.Commerce and references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering — never Aonik.Finance.

1. Summary

AONIK can already catalog, price, stock, sell, bill, and fund a finished product — that is what Spec 042 delivered (a retail storefront on the unified Order spine). What it cannot yet describe is how a finished product is made: the raw materials it consumes and in what quantity. A food-production tenant selling "jollof rice" and "seared steak" has no way to say a portion of jollof is 0.25 kg rice + 0.12 kg tomato + …. Without that, none of the operations-manager workflow — prep lists, ingredient stock, reorder alerts, kitchen sheets, food cost, margin — can exist.

This spec adds the missing master data as two new anemic entities in Aonik.Commerce:

  • Ingredient — a stocked, consumable raw material (rice, tomato, steak cut), with a base unit of measure. Not a saleable Product; it is what products are made from.
  • Recipe + RecipeComponent — the bill of materials for a finished ProductVariant: the list of ingredients and the quantity of each that one yield-unit (a portion) consumes.
Success signals An operator (or the commerce-agent) can define ingredients with a base unit, attach a recipe to a saleable product variant, and read back the exploded bill of materials for a given number of portions ("40 portions of jollof rice ⇒ 10 kg rice, 5 kg tomato, …"). dotnet build Aonik.sln stays green with Aonik.Commerce referencing only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering; dotnet test Aonik.sln stays green with new unit tests covering recipe definition, validation, and explosion.

2. Context & motivation

The driver is a concrete day-in-the-life of a food-production operations manager: they review orders pulled in from the website, read an aggregated production sheet (this week: 40 portions jollof, 30 portions steak), consult an ingredient prep list (10 kg rice, 5 kg tomato…), react to a low-stock alert, place a reorder, print a kitchen sheet, review a profit report (actual food cost vs. target margin), and update ingredient costs when suppliers reprice. That is classic light-manufacturing / back-office ERP — the make-side of commerce, distinct from the retail sell-side Spec 042 shipped.

Every one of those artefacts is a projection of one primitive: a finished good is made of raw materials in known quantities. The production sheet is order demand aggregated by product; the prep list is that demand exploded through recipes; food cost is the recipe valued at ingredient cost; the kitchen sheet is a production run's recipes made concrete. So the recipe is the keystone — this spec builds it first, deliberately excluding cost (Spec 051) and stock (Spec 052) to stay small and independently shippable.

Scope discipline is explicit: build the thin, opinionated maker-operations slice this persona needs — not a general MRP/ERP with multi-level BOMs, routings, work centres, or lot genealogy. Those are noted as open extensions, not built.

5. Scope (in / out)

In scope

  • Ingredient entity + IIngredientService (create / update / list / deactivate) + admin endpoints.
  • Recipe + RecipeComponent entities + IRecipeService (define / replace a variant's recipe, get, list, validate) + admin endpoints.
  • Recipe explosion: given a ProductVariant and a portion count, return the per-ingredient required quantity in each ingredient's base unit.
  • Validation: components reference active ingredients; quantities positive; one active recipe per variant; recipe target is a real variant in the tenant.
  • New Commerce DbSets on CommerceDbContext + AonikDbContext; one tool-generated migration (CommerceRecipesAndIngredients).
  • Commerce-agent read tools for ingredients/recipe + medium-gated write tools for create-ingredient / set-recipe.

Out of scope

  • Ingredient cost and product standard-cost rollup — Spec 051.
  • Ingredient stock, reorder points, low-stock alerts — Spec 052.
  • Multi-level BOMs (a sub-recipe as a component of another recipe), routings/work-centres, yield loss/wastage factors, lot/batch genealogy, allergen roll-ups — noted under Open, not built.
  • Unit-of-measure conversion engine (e.g. buy in 25 kg sacks, stock in kg) — v1 stores quantities in each ingredient's single base unit; purchase-pack conversion lands with procurement (Spec 053).

6. Constraints & guardrails

  • No reference to Finance. Aonik.Commerce still references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering. This spec adds no cross-module coupling (ADR-005).
  • Anemic entities. Ingredient/Recipe/RecipeComponent are { get; set; } data containers implementing ITenantScoped + AuditableEntity (ADR-002); all logic lives in services returning DTOs.
  • Single migration stream. EF-CLI-generated against AonikDbContext; Ank-prefixed tables; CommerceDbContext is runtime scoping with no migrations; agents never hand-author migrations.
  • An ingredient is not a product. A Recipe draws down ingredient stock; a Spec 042 Bundle draws down saleable ProductVariant stock. The two never collapse (§9).
  • Agents propose; systems execute. Ingredient/recipe reads are direct agent tools; writes are medium (in-session confirmation, args-hash-bound); every AI action records an AiRun.
  • Recipes are versionless in v1 but auditable. Replacing a recipe overwrites its components under the same audited entity; historical valuation stability comes from cost snapshots in Spec 051, not recipe versioning (see Open).

8. Domain model

All entities are anemic, ITenantScoped, inherit AuditableEntity.

class Ingredient      { Guid Id, TenantId; string Name; string? Sku; string BaseUnit; // kg | g | L | ml | each
                        string? Category; bool IsActive; string? Notes }

class Recipe          { Guid Id, TenantId, ProductVariantId; string Name;
                        decimal YieldQuantity;  // how many yield-units one run of this recipe produces
                        string  YieldUnit;      // e.g. "portion"
                        bool    IsActive;        // one active recipe per variant
                        List<RecipeComponent> Components; }

class RecipeComponent { Guid Id, TenantId, RecipeId, IngredientId;
                        decimal Quantity;        // in the ingredient's BaseUnit, per YieldQuantity yield-units
                        string? Notes; }

Recipe.ProductVariantId is a real intra-module reference to the Spec 042 ProductVariant being produced (the recipe's "output"). YieldQuantity/YieldUnit let a recipe describe a batch — "this recipe yields 10 portions" — while components are quantities per that yield; explosion (§10) scales to any requested portion count. RecipeComponent.Quantity is always expressed in the referenced ingredient's BaseUnit.

9. Recipe vs. Bundle — the key distinction

Spec 042 already has a "made of other things" concept — the Bundle / build-your-own-box. It is deliberately not reused here, because it models a different thing:

Bundle (Spec 042 §12)Recipe (this spec)
Made ofOther saleable ProductVariants the buyer picksNon-saleable Ingredients an operator defines
Chosen byThe customer, at cart time (selection slots)The operator, once, as master data
Draws downComponent variant stockIngredient stock (Spec 052)
Exists toSell a curated box as one lineDescribe how a product is produced

Keeping them separate avoids overloading BundleSlot/CartItemSelection with production semantics and keeps a single, honest source of inventory truth per concept. A product can be both a bundle and have components produced from recipes — they compose without conflict.

10. Units of measure

Each Ingredient declares one BaseUnit (a free string with a known-values helper: kg, g, L, ml, each). All recipe quantities and (in later specs) stock levels and costs for that ingredient are expressed in its base unit — there is no conversion engine in v1. If a supplier sells rice in 25 kg sacks but the base unit is kg, the pack-to-base factor is a procurement concern handled in Spec 053 (SupplierIngredient.PackSize), not here. Mixing units for one ingredient is simply disallowed; pick the base unit the kitchen actually meters in.

11. Recipe explosion (how downstream specs consume this)

The one piece of logic this spec owns beyond CRUD is explosion — the reusable primitive the prep list (055), production consumption (056), and food cost (051) all call:

IRecipeService.ExplodeAsync(productVariantId, portions) → resolve the variant's single active Recipe → scale each RecipeComponent.Quantity by (portions / Recipe.YieldQuantity) → return [ { IngredientId, Ingredient.Name, BaseUnit, RequiredQuantity } ] IRecipeService.ExplodeManyAsync( [ {variantId, portions}, … ] ) // for a whole production sheet → explode each, then sum RequiredQuantity per IngredientId → return the merged bill of materials ("40 jollof + 30 steak ⇒ 10kg rice, 5kg tomato, …")

Explosion is pure and read-only: it never touches stock or cost (those specs decorate the result). A variant with no active recipe explodes to an empty bill with a diagnostic flag so callers can surface "no recipe defined" rather than silently under-counting.

13. CommerceDbContext & migrations

Three new Ank-prefixed tables — AnkIngredients, AnkRecipes, AnkRecipeComponents — are created by one EF-CLI migration against AonikDbContext. Each entity is added to both CommerceDbContext (runtime scoping) and AonikDbContext (canonical stream); all are tenant-scoped (none global). Configurations live in Persistence/Configurations/Sourcing and …/Production as IEntityTypeConfiguration<T> classes. 

dotnet ef migrations add CommerceRecipesAndIngredients --project src/Aonik.Infrastructure --startup-project src/Aonik.Api

14. Phased rollout

Phase 1 — Ingredient master

A list of raw materials with base units.
  • Ingredient entity + config + DbSets; IIngredientService (create/update/list/deactivate) returning DTOs; admin endpoints; the CommerceRecipesAndIngredients migration (this phase creates AnkIngredients; recipe tables land with Phase 2 in the same migration if shipped together, else a second migration).

Phase 2 — Recipe / bill of materials + explosion

Attach a recipe to a product; read the exploded BOM.
  • Recipe/RecipeComponent + configs; IRecipeService (set/get/list + validation + ExplodeAsync/ExplodeManyAsync); admin endpoints; unit tests for validation and explosion scaling.

Phase 3 — Commerce-agent tools

The agentic surface.
  • Read tools (list ingredients, get recipe, explode) + medium-gated write tools (create ingredient, set recipe) added to CommerceAgentTools and classified in CommerceToolApprovalManifest; routed through IToolApprovalGate.GateAll (fails closed).

17. Verification

  • dotnet build Aonik.sln green; architecture test proves Aonik.Commerce has no Aonik.Finance reference.
  • Explosion: define ingredients (rice kg, tomato kg), a jollof recipe (yield 4 portions ⇒ 1 kg rice, 0.5 kg tomato), a steak recipe; ExplodeManyAsync([{jollof,40},{steak,30}]) returns 10 kg rice, 5 kg tomato, plus the steak components — matching the day-in-the-life prep list.
  • Validation: a component with a deactivated ingredient, a non-positive quantity, or a second active recipe on the same variant is rejected.
  • Agent: commerce_set_recipe surfaces a medium approval; commerce_explode_recipe executes directly; each records an AiRun.
  • dotnet test Aonik.sln green (xUnit + FluentAssertions, InMemory + test tenant/user providers).

19. Decisions baked in

  • Ingredient is a first-class entity, distinct from saleable Product — the maker's raw-material master.
  • Recipe = bill of materials attached to a ProductVariant; components carry quantity per yield in the ingredient's base unit.
  • Explosion is the shared primitive the prep list, production consumption, and food cost all reuse.
  • Recipe ≠ Bundle; ingredient stock ≠ variant stock.
  • Grow in place in Aonik.Commerce (no new module); no Finance reference; migrations stay with AonikDbContext.
  • v1 is single-level, single-unit, versionless — deliberately thin; extensions are Open.

20. Open design decisions

Open Recipe versioning. Overwrite-in-place (v1) vs. an append-only RecipeVersion with an effective date, so a production run records the recipe version it used. Deferred; cost/stock snapshots cover historical valuation in the interim.
Open Multi-level BOM. Allowing a sub-recipe (a semi-finished good) as a component of another recipe. v1 is single-level; the explosion algorithm is written so a recursive expansion can be added without changing callers.
Open Yield loss / wastage factor. A per-component or per-recipe wastage multiplier (e.g. 5% trim loss). Not in v1; would decorate explosion.
Open Unit-of-measure conversions. A conversion table so an ingredient can be metered in one unit and purchased in another. v1 uses a single base unit; procurement pack-size (Spec 053) handles the buy-side case.
