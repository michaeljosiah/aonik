Spec 057 Status: Draft Size: Small Priority: P3 Domain: Commerce / Reporting

Margin & profit report — revenue vs. COGS vs. target margin

The profit-report moment that closes the operations-manager's day: put actual sales revenue next to what the food cost to make, and flag where next week's plan needs adjusting. This spec is a thin read / reporting layer plus one tiny additive schema change. It sits on the Spec 050 keystone (recipes) and its cost rollup Spec 051, and reads revenue straight from the Order spine / Spec 042 charge data. Everything lives in Aonik.Commerce and references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering — never Aonik.Finance. This is the last spec of the maker-operations family (050–057); nothing depends on it.

1. Summary

The maker-operations family (050–057) has taught AONIK what a product is made of (050), what it costs to make (051), how much raw material is on hand (052), how to reorder and receive (053/054), and how to plan and run production (055/056). The last question the operations manager asks each week is: did we make money, and on what?

This spec answers it with a single read service, IMarginReportService, that for a date window computes — per ProductVariant and in aggregate — revenue (from payment-completed ProductPurchase orders' lines, §8), COGS (the Spec 051 standard-cost rollup times the quantity sold), the resulting gross margin and margin percentage, and a variance flag against a target margin. The only persisted change is one additive nullable column, TargetMarginPct, on the existing Spec 042 Product.

Success signals An operator (or the commerce-agent) can ask "how did last week look?" and get a per-dish profit table — revenue, food cost, gross margin, margin %, and a red flag on any variant below its target — plus the aggregate for the window. Setting a target margin on a product records an AiRun. dotnet build Aonik.sln stays green with Aonik.Commerce referencing only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering; dotnet test Aonik.sln stays green with unit tests covering revenue aggregation, COGS valuation, margin/variance, and the no-target and no-recipe edge cases.

2. Context & motivation

In the day-in-the-life that drives this family, the operations manager ends the loop by reviewing a profit report: they compare the actual food cost of what was sold to the target margins they set, and decide where next week's plan needs adjusting — drop a low-margin dish, reprice it, or renegotiate an ingredient. That decision closes the loop back to production planning (Spec 055) and, ultimately, to updating ingredient costs (Spec 051).

Every input already exists. Revenue is captured by the Order spine at checkout — Spec 042 records the funded, discounted total as a durable OrderChargeSummary (see §8). Food cost is the standard cost Spec 051 rolls up from the recipe. So the profit report is a projection over data AONIK already holds: this spec adds no ledger, no new money movement, and (bar one nullable column) no new schema — just the read service that joins revenue to cost per variant and measures it against a target.

Scope discipline: this is standard-cost margin, not managerial accounting. Actual (goods-receipt-valued) COGS, labour/overhead, period-close snapshots, and margin sliced by channel or customer tier are noted under Open, not built.

4. Scope (in / out)

In scope

  • IMarginReportService: for a window, per ProductVariant and aggregate — revenue, COGS, gross margin, margin %, and target-variance flag.
  • Revenue from payment-completed ProductPurchase orders' lines using the Spec 042 OrderChargeSummary discounted total (apportioned pro-rata, §8), read via the Ordering/charge data (no Finance reference).
  • COGS = RollupStandardCostAsync(variant, currency, atUtc) (Spec 051) × quantity sold in the window.
  • One additive nullable column TargetMarginPct on the existing Product entity; a set-target write path (SetTargetMarginAsync, 0–100 range-validated, null clears).
  • A read endpoint (get margin report for a window + currency) and a PUT set-target endpoint; commerce_get_margin_report read tool and commerce_set_target_margin medium tool.
  • One tool-generated migration (CommerceTargetMargin) adding the single column.

Out of scope

  • Actual (goods-receipt-based) COGS — valuing sold quantity at real received cost (Spec 054 lots) rather than standard cost. v1 is standard-cost; noted Open.
  • Labour & overhead beyond food cost — v1 margin is food-cost margin only.
  • Period close & snapshotting a report as an immutable artefact; v1 recomputes live from current cost + order data.
  • Margin sliced by category / channel / customer tier, and linking a margin variance back to a Spec 055 plan adjustment — noted Open.
  • A separate ProductMarginTarget entity — v1 uses the column on Product; the richer entity is Open.

5. Constraints & guardrails

  • No reference to Finance. Revenue is read from the Ordering spine (ProductPurchase lines + OrderChargeSummary), not from the ledger. Aonik.Commerce still references only SharedKernel + Aonik.Ordering (ADR-005).
  • Revenue is the discounted total of payment-completed orders. The report uses the funded amount from OrderChargeSummary (subtotal − discount, per Spec 042 §17), never gross list price, and counts only orders whose status is Complete — the transition payment completion applies (§8) — so margin reflects money that actually arrived.
  • COGS is standard cost. v1 values sold quantity at the Spec 051 rollup, not receipt-based actual cost, keeping revenue/COGS timing honest without lot valuation (§9, Risks).
  • Read-only bar one column. The service computes and returns DTOs; the only mutation is setting Product.TargetMarginPct. No new money movement, no ledger, no OrderChargeSummary writes.
  • Single migration stream. The one additive column is EF-CLI-generated against AonikDbContext; Ank table; CommerceDbContext is runtime scoping with no migrations; agents never hand-author migrations.
  • Agents propose; systems execute. The margin report is a direct read tool; setting a target margin is a medium write (in-session confirmation, args-hash-bound); every AI action records an AiRun.

7. Domain model

No new persisted entity. The one schema change is a nullable column on the existing Spec 042 Product; everything else is computed DTOs returned by the service. 

// EXTEND — Spec 042 catalog entity, anemic, ITenantScoped, AuditableEntity
class Product { /* …existing Spec 042 fields… */
                decimal? TargetMarginPct;   // NEW — target gross-margin %, 0–100, precision (5,2); null = no target set
              }

// Computed DTOs (not persisted; Contracts/Models/Reporting). The window record is Spec 055's
// ProductionWindow — the landed half-open [FromUtc, ToUtc) primitive, reused not duplicated.
record MarginReportRowDto { Guid ProductVariantId; string ProductName, VariantName;
                            decimal QuantitySold;
                            decimal Revenue;        // DISCOUNTED goods revenue attributed to the variant (§8)
                            decimal? Cogs;          // RollupStandardCost × QuantitySold; null ⇒ COGS unknown
                            decimal? GrossMargin;   // Revenue − Cogs (null when Cogs unknown)
                            decimal? MarginPct;     // 0–100 scale, 2 dp (null when Revenue = 0 or COGS unknown)
                            bool     CogsKnown;     // false ⇒ no recipe / no component cost — excluded from aggregate
                            bool     IsBundle;      // row includes bundle attribution (component-expanded, §8)
                            decimal? TargetMarginPct;
                            bool?    BelowTarget; } // MarginPct < TargetMarginPct; NULL when either is unknown

record MarginAggregateDto { decimal Revenue;             // ALL rows (incl. unknown-COGS rows)
                            decimal KnownCogsRevenue;    // COGS-known rows only — the margin denominator
                            decimal Cogs, GrossMargin;   // COGS-known rows only
                            decimal? MarginPct;          // over KnownCogsRevenue ONLY; 0–100 scale, 2 dp
                            decimal UnknownCogsRevenue; }// = Revenue − KnownCogsRevenue: surfaced, excluded

record MarginReportDto    { ProductionWindow Window; string Currency;
                            IReadOnlyList<MarginReportRowDto> Rows;   // incl. unknown-COGS rows
                            MarginAggregateDto Aggregate;
                            IReadOnlyList<Guid> VariantsWithoutRecipe;    // Spec 050 diagnostic
                            IReadOnlyList<Guid> VariantsWithUnknownCost;  // Spec 051 diagnostic (recipe, no cost)
                            int OrdersExcludedByCurrency; }               // other-currency orders: skipped, surfaced

record TargetMarginDto    { Guid ProductId; string ProductName; decimal? TargetMarginPct; }

Cogs, GrossMargin, and MarginPct are all nullable because a variant with sales but no computable cost (no active recipe, or a component with no active cost) must be surfaced as a diagnostic rather than a misleading zero (§9): its CogsKnown = false row is excluded from the aggregate, never counted as zero cost — the two diagnostics lists say why (no recipe vs. recipe with a missing component cost). MarginPct is expressed on the 0–100 percentage scale (2 dp, away-from-zero) so it compares directly against TargetMarginPct; BelowTarget is nullable — null means "cannot judge" (no target set, or margin unknown), never a false-negative false. IsBundle marks rows carrying build-your-own-box attribution — a component variant expanded from a bundle line, or an unexpanded bundle line surfaced as its own flagged row (§8). TargetMarginPct lives on Product (not ProductVariant): a target is a product-level commercial intent, and the row carries the resolved value so callers need not re-join.

8. Revenue source (reusing OrderChargeSummary)

Revenue is read from the Order spine, not the ledger — keeping the Commerce/Finance boundary intact. For each qualifying ProductPurchase order (decision below), the service reads the line-level goods (OrderItem.ProductId / variant, Quantity, AmountIn — the line total as checkout wrote it) via IOrderService.ListWithItemsAsync (the Spec 055 read path, type + window pushed into the query) and the order's durable OrderChargeSummary — the record Spec 042 §17 persists at checkout capturing subtotal → discount → tax → payable, funded via the PaymentIntent.

Decision — which orders count (grounded) This is a profit report: only payment-completed orders count as revenue. The landed lifecycle: checkout creates the order in Draft and writes OrderChargeSummary.PaymentStatus once, with the intent's initial status — nothing ever updates that column. Payment completion is signalled on the spine: PaymentCompletedEvent → CommercePaymentCompletedHandler → ConfirmPaymentAsync commits inventory, closes the cart, and transitions the order to Complete. So the honest "money actually arrived" predicate is Order.Status = Complete, not the charge summary's snapshot field. v1 therefore counts ProductPurchase orders with status Complete, created in the half-open UTC window [FromUtc, ToUtc) — CreatedAt is the spine's only windowable instant on the read contract; completion-time windowing would need a status-history read and is deliberately not v1. This is narrower than Spec 055's demand set (Pending / UnderReview / Approved / Transmitted / Complete): the kitchen cooks for committed orders, but profit counts only funded ones — a Pending order appears on the production sheet and not in the margin report. Orders in a currency other than the requested report currency are skipped and surfaced (OrdersExcludedByCurrency) — Commerce holds no FX; ₦ and £ are never summed.
Decision Revenue uses the discounted total from OrderChargeSummary (subtotal minus discount), not gross list price. That is the amount actually funded, so margin reflects what the customer paid. Where a whole-order discount spans multiple lines, the order-level DiscountTotal is apportioned to the lines pro-rata by line AmountIn, each share rounded to 4 dp away-from-zero with the rounding remainder assigned to the largest-weight row (ties → first) — so per-variant revenue sums to the order's discounted total exactly, never off by a rounding tail. A Complete order with no charge summary (created outside Commerce checkout) contributes its line amounts undiscounted — there is no discount record to apportion.

Tax is excluded from margin revenue (tax is a pass-through liability, not the maker's income). The read goes through the Ordering read surface — no Aonik.Finance reference and no ledger query, per §5.

Decision — bundle expansion (grounded) Bundle lines must be detected, not silently mis-joined. Per Spec 042 §12 (Option A), a build-your-own-box sale stores OrderItem.ProductId = the bundle Product id (Kind = Bundle), not a variant, with the chosen contents in OrderBundleSelection rows — so the report's ProductId → ProductVariant → Recipe join will not resolve for a bundle line. Detection mirrors the landed Spec 055 sheet rule: a line with OrderBundleSelection rows (keyed OrderId + OrderItemIndex; selection Quantity is already the line total, selection × boxes) is expanded into its components, attributing the line's discounted revenue across them and valuing each at its Spec 051 rollup — so bundle revenue/COGS group by real recipe rather than dropping or mis-joining. v1 split key (grounded): OrderBundleSelection persists no price snapshot, so the line's revenue is split pro-rata by relative standalone selling price — each component's current active list price in the report currency (IProductPricingService.ResolvePriceAsync) × selection quantity — falling back to pro-rata by quantity when any component price is unresolvable; the same 4 dp/remainder rounding as the discount apportionment, so component revenue still sums to the line's revenue exactly. v1-minimum fallback: a bundle line without selection rows keys a row on the bundle product id that is flagged (IsBundle = true, CogsKnown = false, excluded from the aggregate margin) — never silently attributed. Refining the split key (checkout-time price snapshot, explicit weighting) stays Open.

9. COGS from standard cost (Spec 051)

Cost of goods sold is standard cost: for each variant sold in the window, the service calls the Spec 051 rollup and multiplies by the quantity sold.

for each ProductVariant sold in [FromUtc, ToUtc): // Complete (payment-completed) orders only, §8 qty = Σ OrderItem.Quantity (+ bundle-selection quantities) // ProductPurchase lines created in window unitCogs = IProductCostingService.RollupStandardCostAsync(variantId, currency, atUtc: now) = Σ (RecipeComponent.Quantity × active IngredientCost.UnitCost) // Spec 051; live standard cost Cogs = unitCogs unknown ? null : round4(unitCogs × qty) // no recipe / no component cost ⇒ CogsKnown = false GrossMargin = Cogs is null ? null : Revenue − Cogs MarginPct = (Revenue = 0 || Cogs is null) ? null : round2(GrossMargin / Revenue × 100) // 0–100 scale BelowTarget = (MarginPct is set && TargetMarginPct is set) ? MarginPct < TargetMarginPct : null // rows with CogsKnown = false are surfaced but EXCLUDED from Aggregate.Cogs / Aggregate.MarginPct; // their revenue is Aggregate.UnknownCogsRevenue (= Revenue − KnownCogsRevenue)

If a variant has no active recipe (or a recipe component with no active cost — reusing the Spec 050/051 "no recipe / no cost" diagnostic), the rollup yields no cost. The row then sets CogsKnown = false with Cogs left null (not zero) and is surfaced as "COGS unknown" — the report's VariantsWithoutRecipe / VariantsWithUnknownCost lists say which of the two diagnoses applies — so the operator sees "cost unknown — define a recipe" rather than a phantom 100% margin. Such a row is excluded from the aggregate — its revenue is reported under Aggregate.UnknownCogsRevenue so coverage gaps are visible — and never folded into Aggregate.Cogs or Aggregate.MarginPct as zero cost, which would inflate reported profit (the aggregate margin's denominator is KnownCogsRevenue, not all revenue). Rolling up in the report currency also gives the "one currency" rule for free: an ingredient costed only in another currency makes CostComplete = false — the row is skipped-and-flagged, never FX-converted. To keep revenue and cost on the same footing, COGS values the quantity sold in the window at the live standard cost (a documented choice; see Risks for the timing nuance vs. actual receipt cost).

10. Target margin & variance

A Product may carry a target gross-margin percentage (TargetMarginPct, nullable, 0–100 on the same percentage scale as MarginPct). The report resolves it onto each variant row and sets BelowTarget = true when the achieved MarginPct falls below it — the single signal the operator scans to decide what next week's plan must change. BelowTarget is null — "cannot judge", never a reassuring false — when the target is unset or the achieved margin is unknown (zero revenue / unknown COGS). Setting the target is the only write in this spec: IMarginReportService.SetTargetMarginAsync(productId, pct?) — the margin feature service owns its commercial write, mirroring how SetPriceAsync lives on the pricing service — validating the 0–100 range and storing at 2 dp (the column's (5,2) precision, enforced in the service because InMemory tests cannot prove DB precision); null clears. Exposed as a PUT admin endpoint and the commerce_set_target_margin medium tool (§12), recorded as an AiRun. A separate ProductMarginTarget entity — with effective dates or per-channel targets — is deferred (Open); v1 keeps one current target on the product.

11. The profit-report projection

The whole spec is one projection over data AONIK already holds — revenue from the spine, cost from the rollup, target from the product — assembled per variant and summed:

IMarginReportService.GetMarginReportAsync(window, currency) // ProductionWindow: [FromUtc, ToUtc) → gather payment-completed (Status = Complete) ProductPurchase orders created in the window (§8) → join each order's OrderChargeSummary → apportion DiscountTotal pro-rata by line AmountIn (§8) → expand bundle lines through their OrderBundleSelection rows (split by component list value) (§8) → per ProductVariant: QuantitySold, Revenue (discounted, apportioned, reconciling exactly) → value each at RollupStandardCostAsync → Cogs, GrossMargin, MarginPct (CogsKnown = false ⇒ excluded) → resolve Product.TargetMarginPct → BelowTarget flag (null when either side unknown) → MarginReportDto { Window, Currency, Rows[], Aggregate { Revenue, KnownCogsRevenue, Cogs, GrossMargin, MarginPct, UnknownCogsRevenue }, VariantsWithoutRecipe[], VariantsWithUnknownCost[], OrdersExcludedByCurrency } IMarginReportService.SetTargetMarginAsync(productId, pct?) // the spec's ONE write; null clears "Last week: jollof sold 40 @ discounted revenue, food cost from recipe → 68% margin (target 70% ⇒ below); steak 30 → 55% margin (target 50% ⇒ ok); aggregate 62%."

The projection is pure and read-only: it computes from current cost and order data on demand and persists nothing (period-close snapshotting is Open). Every downstream artefact the operator sees — the per-dish table, the aggregate, the below-target flags — is a view of this one call.

17. Verification

  • dotnet build Aonik.sln green; architecture test proves Aonik.Commerce has no Aonik.Finance reference.
  • Margin: a variant sold 40 units, discounted revenue R, standard cost C per unit ⇒ row reports Revenue = R, Cogs = 40C, GrossMargin = R−40C, MarginPct = (R−40C)/R × 100 (the Spec 051 jollof economics: 40 portions at ₦2,000 on a ₦400/portion cost ⇒ 80%); a product target above that margin sets BelowTarget = true.

  • Agent: commerce_get_margin_report executes directly; commerce_set_target_margin surfaces a medium approval; each records an AiRun.
  • dotnet test Aonik.sln green (xUnit + FluentAssertions, InMemory + test tenant/user providers).

19. Decisions baked in

  • Margin report is a live read projection over data AONIK already holds — no ledger, no new money movement, no snapshot.
  • Revenue counts payment-completed orders only (Status = Complete, the transition ConfirmPaymentAsync applies on PaymentCompletedEvent), created in the half-open CreatedAt window — deliberately narrower than Spec 055's demand set (§8). OrderChargeSummary.PaymentStatus is a checkout-time snapshot, never updated, and is NOT the completion signal.
  • Revenue = discounted total from OrderChargeSummary (Spec 042), read via the Ordering spine — never a Finance ledger query; the order-level discount is apportioned pro-rata by line amount (4 dp away-from-zero, remainder to the largest row) so rows reconcile to the funded total exactly; tax excluded.
  • COGS = standard cost (Spec 051 rollup, live, in the report currency) × quantity sold; unknown COGS is null and excluded from the aggregate, never zero; actual receipt-cost valuation is deferred.
  • Bundle revenue splits by relative standalone selling price (current active list price × selection quantity; quantity fallback) across OrderBundleSelection components — the selections persist no price snapshot (§8).
  • Target margin is a nullable column on Product, not a new entity; variance is a single nullable BelowTarget flag (null = cannot judge); the set-target write lives on IMarginReportService, mirroring SetPrice-on-the-pricing-service.
  • Grow in place in Aonik.Commerce; no Finance reference; the one migration stays with AonikDbContext.
  • Read tool direct; set-target medium; every AI action records an AiRun.

20. Open design decisions

Open Actual (goods-receipt-based) COGS. Valuing sold quantity at real received cost from Spec 054 lots (FIFO/average) instead of standard cost, giving a true-cost margin and a purchase-price-variance line. v1 is standard-cost; the DTO leaves room for an ActualCogs column alongside Cogs.
Open Labour & overhead. Extending margin beyond food cost to include prep labour and fixed overhead allocation (a fully-loaded contribution margin). Not in v1; would decorate the COGS term.
Open Period close & snapshotting. Persisting a report as an immutable, dated artefact (so a closed week doesn't move when costs later change) vs. always recomputing live (v1). A MarginReportSnapshot entity would land here.
Open Bundle-component margin attribution. v1 landed on pro-rata by current component list price × quantity (relative standalone selling price, with a quantity fallback — §8), because OrderBundleSelection persists no checkout-time price. Refinements land here: snapshotting component prices onto the selections at checkout (so a later reprice cannot shift historical attribution), splitting by component standard cost or an explicit bundle-tier weighting, and whether the parent bundle is also reported as a rolled-up row alongside its components. Completion-time windowing (vs. the v1 CreatedAt window, §8) would also land here, via a status-history read.
Open Slicing & separate target entity. Margin sliced by category / sales channel / customer tier, and a richer ProductMarginTarget entity (effective-dated, per-channel targets) instead of the single TargetMarginPct column.
Open Closing the loop to planning. Linking a below-target margin variance back to a concrete Spec 055 plan adjustment (drop / reprice / re-source a dish), so the profit report drives next week's production sheet rather than just reporting last week's.
