Spec 008 Status: Draft Size: Medium Priority: P1 Domain: Finance / Platform

Recipients

A recipient is who a customer sends money to — the person or business on the receiving end of a transfer, remittance, or payout. This spec defines the recipient surface (create, read, list, update, remove, photo) as a thin façade over the party graph that already exists in the codebase: a recipient is a Party reached by a directed Recipient relationship edge, marked Beneficiary for its owning customer, with one or more saved payout destinations. It is not a standalone table. The original 08-line stub proposed a generic Recipient CRUD entity; the platform has since chosen the party-graph model, and this spec is rewritten to match the code as built.

1. Summary

Recipients are the address book of a money-movement product. Before a customer can send a remittance (Payabo's UK→NG launch wedge), pay a bill on someone's behalf, or schedule a payout, the platform needs a durable, reusable record of who is being paid and how to reach them — a name, a relationship, optionally a face, and one or more payout rails (a bank account, a mobile-money wallet). This is the concept the legacy MyBillAfrica/MTM products called “friends” or “recipients.”

AONIK already models this, but not as a Recipient entity. It is assembled from primitives that exist for good architectural reasons:

  • the Party / PersonProfile subsystem in Platform (identity, display name, photo);
  • a directed PartyRelationship edge of type Recipient from the customer to the payee;
  • a PartyRoleAssignment of Beneficiary scoped to that customer;
  • one or more ExternalPayoutAccount rows in Finance holding the masked destination + the connector's reusable token.

Two operations are already shipped — PayoutBeneficiaryService's SaveBeneficiaryAsync (POST /payments/payout-beneficiaries) and ListBeneficiariesAsync (GET /payments/payout-beneficiaries/{customerPartyId}). This spec keeps that substrate exactly as-is and defines the rest of the recipient surface on top of it: a customer-facing /recipients resource with get-one, update, remove, search + paging, and photo upload — the parts the original stub named but the code has not yet grown.

The reframing The stub asked for a Recipient CRUD service over a new table. The platform answered with a party graph: reusable identity in Platform, payable rails in Finance, joined by a relationship edge and a role. This spec ratifies that decision and closes the surface gap — it does not introduce a competing Recipient table.

2. A recipient is a party, not a record

The single most important constraint, already honoured in code, is that a recipient has no identity of its own. “Recipient” is a role a party plays relative to a customer, not a thing that exists in isolation. This buys three properties the platform depends on:

  • Reuse & dedup. The same underlying party can be a customer's recipient, a household member, and the payee on a historical order — one identity, many relationships. SaveBeneficiaryAsync already takes an optional BeneficiaryPartyId to reuse an existing party rather than minting a duplicate.
  • History integrity. Orders reference parties by a soft Guid (Order.PayerPartyId, OrderPartyRole.PartyId) with no foreign key. “Removing a recipient” can therefore deactivate the relationship without ever touching, cascading into, or invalidating a past order.
  • One graph, many products. The same edge powers Payabo remittance, MyBillAfrica bill-collection payees, and RemitExchange beneficiaries. A bespoke Recipient table would fork that graph per product.
Already true in the code PayoutBeneficiaryService writes the party, the customer→recipient edge, and the Beneficiary role through the cross-module IPartyService seam, then persists the rail in Finance — and makes the edge/role idempotent so saving a second rail for the same recipient does not duplicate the relationship. This spec's IRecipientService is a façade over exactly these moves.

3. Current state & the gap

What the original stub listed, against what exists in the codebase today:

Stub operationStatusWhere it lives / what's missing

GET /recipients (list) Shipped no search/paging GET /payments/payout-beneficiaries/{customerPartyId} → ListBeneficiariesAsync. Returns all of a customer's saved rails; has no search, paging, or sort yet.

PUT /recipients/{recipientId} (update) Gap No update path. Editing display name / relationship / notes is a party + edge update; editing a rail is an ExternalPayoutAccount update. (Re-saving the same rail is idempotent today, but there is no field-level edit.)
DELETE /recipients/{recipientId} (remove) Gap No removal path. Must be a soft removal: deactivate the relationship edge (and optionally soft-delete the rail), never delete the party or historical orders (§9).
POST /recipients/{recipientId}/photo Gap machinery exists No recipient photo path, but the mechanism exists: IProfilePhotoStore already uploads a party photo (original + 3 thumbnails) keyed by (tenantId, partyId) onto PersonProfile.PhotoUrl* (§8).
Route convergence is a real decision Today the operator route is /payments/payout-beneficiaries. The product noun is recipient. This spec introduces a customer-facing /recipients resource as the canonical surface and treats the two shipped beneficiary endpoints as the same operations under an operator-flavoured route. Whether to alias, redirect, or retire the older route is an open decision (§14) — not a forced rename in this spec.

4. Scope

In scope

  • An IRecipientService façade composing IPartyService + IPayoutBeneficiaryService + the party-photo seam, returning a unified RecipientResponse (identity + relationship + photo URLs + saved rails).
  • The full /recipients REST surface: create, get-one, list (with search + paging), update, remove, and photo upload.
  • Search + paging on the list (by display name; page/pageSize; stable sort).
  • Recipient photo upload reusing IProfilePhotoStore, plus a read-contract addition so a recipient projection can return the photo URL.
  • Soft-removal semantics that preserve the party and all historical orders.

Out of scope

  • A new Recipient table or entity. Explicitly rejected (§2).
  • Payout execution / money movement — that is Orders + Payments (Spec 005, Spec 006), not this resource. A recipient is a destination, not a transaction.
  • Beneficiary verification (name-match / penny-drop) — ExternalPayoutAccount.IsVerified exists as a flag, but the verification flow is a connector concern (Spec 031).
  • Storing raw account numbers / MSISDNs / wallet ids. Only the masked identifier + the connector's reusable token are ever persisted (Spec 031 sensitive-data rule).
  • Sharing recipients across customers/tenants. The Beneficiary role is scoped to one owning customer; there is no global address book.

5. Domain model

Nothing here is new. A recipient is the composition of five existing entities across two modules, joined by soft Guid references:

5.1 The entities (all existing)

EntityModuleRole in a recipient
PartyPlatformThe recipient's identity: PartyType (Person|Business), DisplayName, Status. ITenantScoped.
PersonProfilePlatform1:1 detail for a Person recipient: names, country, and the four photo URLs (PhotoUrl, PhotoUrlMedium, PhotoUrlSmall, PhotoUrlTiny).
PartyRelationshipPlatformDirected edge customer→recipient with RelationshipTypeCode = "Recipient" (or a kinship code) and an IsActive flag — the removal hook.
PartyRoleAssignmentPlatformMarks the recipient Beneficiary in the Customer context of its owner — “payable by this customer.”
ExternalPayoutAccountFinanceA saved rail: masked identifier + AccountName + Currency + bank/mobile metadata + the connector's reusable ProviderBeneficiaryId token. A recipient may have several.

Relationship codes come from PartyRelationshipTypes (validated set: Self, Mother, Father, Spouse, Sibling, Child, Friend, Business, Other, Recipient). The neutral Recipient code is the cross-module constant PartyRelationshipTypeCodes.Recipient so Finance creates the edge with the exact code Platform's validator accepts. A recipient that is also kin (e.g. you remit to your mother) carries the kinship edge and is payable — the list de-dups by recipient party and keeps the display edge.

6. API surface

The canonical resource is /recipients, scoped to a customer party. The two shipped operations are noted against their current operator route.

Method & routeStatusPurpose
POST /recipients core shipped Create/save a recipient + a rail. Body is the recipient identity (reuse partyId or create) plus the masked destination. Maps to SaveBeneficiaryAsync.
GET /recipients extend List a customer's recipients with ?search=&page=&pageSize=. Generalises ListBeneficiariesAsync with a name filter + paging envelope.
GET /recipients/{recipientPartyId} new One recipient: identity + relationship + photo URLs + saved rails, scoped to the requesting customer.
PUT /recipients/{recipientPartyId} new Update display fields / relationship / notes (party + edge), and/or a named rail (ExternalPayoutAccount).
DELETE /recipients/{recipientPartyId} new Soft-remove: deactivate the relationship edge (and soft-delete its rails). Party + history untouched (§9).
POST /recipients/{recipientPartyId}/photo new Multipart photo upload → original + 3 thumbnails on the recipient's PersonProfile; returns the photo URLs (§8).

Scoping & identity. “Which customer” is resolved server-side: in Payabo the customer party is the authenticated user's party; on the admin surface it is supplied (as the existing list route does with {customerPartyId}). Every read filters to the caller's owned edges — a recipient party id alone is never enough to read another customer's recipient. Routes use the recipient party id as the public identifier (there is no separate recipient id), consistent with the party-graph model.

7. Service design

IRecipientService lives in Finance (alongside IPayoutBeneficiaryService), because the saved rail and the customer-ownership question are Finance concerns and the party seam is already consumed there. It composes three existing seams — it adds no new storage of its own beyond what PayoutBeneficiaryService already writes. 

// src/Aonik.Finance/Contracts/Services/Payments/IRecipientService.cs   (new — façade)
public interface IRecipientService
{
    // POST /recipients — delegates to IPayoutBeneficiaryService.SaveBeneficiaryAsync,
    // then projects the unified view.
    Task<RecipientResponse> CreateAsync(SaveRecipientRequest request, CancellationToken ct = default);

    // GET /recipients/{id}
    Task<RecipientResponse?> GetAsync(Guid customerPartyId, Guid recipientPartyId, CancellationToken ct = default);

    // GET /recipients?search=&page=&pageSize=
    Task<RecipientListResponse> ListAsync(Guid customerPartyId, RecipientQuery query, CancellationToken ct = default);

    // PUT /recipients/{id} — party/edge fields and/or a named rail
    Task<RecipientResponse> UpdateAsync(Guid customerPartyId, Guid recipientPartyId, UpdateRecipientRequest request, CancellationToken ct = default);

    // DELETE /recipients/{id} — soft-remove (deactivate edge + soft-delete rails)
    Task RemoveAsync(Guid customerPartyId, Guid recipientPartyId, CancellationToken ct = default);

    // POST /recipients/{id}/photo — party-photo seam (§8)
    Task<RecipientPhotoResponse> UploadPhotoAsync(
        Guid customerPartyId, Guid recipientPartyId, string contentType, Stream photo, CancellationToken ct = default);
}

// The unified projection the API returns — identity + relationship + photo + rails.
public record RecipientResponse(
    Guid RecipientPartyId,
    string DisplayName,
    string PartyType,                         // Person | Business
    string RelationshipTypeCode,              // Recipient | Mother | …
    string? PhotoUrl,                         // from PersonProfile (§8)
    string? PhotoUrlSmall,
    bool IsActive,
    IReadOnlyList<RecipientRailResponse> Rails);

public record RecipientRailResponse(
    Guid ExternalPayoutAccountId,
    string DestinationType,                   // Bank | MobileMoney | Wallet
    string AccountName,
    string MaskedAccountIdentifier,           // never the raw value
    string Currency,
    string? BankCode,
    string? MobileNetwork,
    bool IsVerified);

CreateAsync and the rail half of UpdateAsync delegate straight to the shipped PayoutBeneficiaryService (idempotent edge/role + rail upsert). GetAsync / ListAsync read the customer's relationship edges via IPartyService.GetRelationshipsAsync, join the Finance rails (as ListBeneficiariesAsync already does), and enrich with the recipient's profile + photo (§8). RemoveAsync is the only genuinely new write path and is a party-edge deactivation (§9).

Reuse, don't re-derive The customer→recipient join, the masked-identifier discipline, and the idempotent edge/role writes already live in PayoutBeneficiaryService. IRecipientService must call into them, not reimplement the graph stitching — otherwise two code paths can drift on the “recipient = party + edge + role + rail” invariant.

8. Recipient photos

Because a recipient is a party, a recipient photo is a party photo — the exact machinery customer profile photos already use. No new storage abstraction is needed:

  • IProfilePhotoStore.UploadCustomerPhotoAsync(tenantId, partyId, contentType, stream) already buffers the upload, validates the image, and produces an original (max 1920×1920) plus 512 / 128 / 64 thumbnails via IImageProcessingService, storing them through the IBlobStorageFactory (local filesystem or Azure Blob) and returning a PhotoUploadResult with all four URLs.
  • The recipient's four PersonProfile.PhotoUrl* columns are the existing landing place for those URLs — no schema change for the photo itself.
  • The upload endpoint mirrors UploadCustomerPhotoEndpoint: AllowFileUploads(), 422 on missing/empty/invalid image, 502 on storage I/O failure.
The one real seam to add The cross-module IPartyService.GetPartyAsync returns a lean PartyResponse(PartyId, DisplayName, PartyType, Status) — no photo URLs. For a recipient projection to return an imageUrl, and for Finance to set a recipient's photo without reaching into Platform's DbContext, the party contract needs a small, additive extension: either a richer read (PartyProfileResponse carrying the photo URLs) or a dedicated SetPartyPhotoAsync(partyId, …) / GetPartyPhotoAsync(partyId) on the Platform-owned seam. Ownership stays in Platform (it owns PersonProfile and IProfilePhotoStore); Finance calls the seam. Pick one shape in §14 before building — this is the only piece that crosses a module boundary.

Secure storage & signed URLs. The blob factory already supports a private Azure container; IProfilePhotoStore.GetPhotoUrl is the indirection point where a short-lived SAS / signed URL is minted when the container is not public. Recipient photos ride the same policy as customer photos — no separate signing path.

9. Lifecycle, tenancy & deletion

  • Tenant-scoped throughout. Party, PartyRelationship, PartyRoleAssignment, and ExternalPayoutAccount are all ITenantScoped; every query filters by the ambient tenant. The acceptance criterion “recipient records are tenant scoped” is satisfied by construction.
  • Removal is soft and edge-level. DELETE /recipients/{id} sets PartyRelationship.IsActive = false for the customer→recipient edge and soft-deletes the recipient's ExternalPayoutAccount rows. The party is never deleted — it may still be kin, another customer's recipient, or the payee on a closed order.
  • Historical orders are untouchable from here. Orders hold soft Guid references to parties with no foreign key (Order.PayerPartyId, OrderPartyRole.PartyId), so removing a recipient cannot cascade into, null out, or invalidate any past order. This is the structural guarantee behind the stub's “deleting a recipient does not delete historical orders.”
  • Re-adding is reactivation. Saving a recipient that maps to an existing edge re-activates it rather than minting a duplicate — consistent with the existing idempotent EnsureRelationshipAsync behaviour.

10. Agent exposure & approval tier

When a domain agent (e.g. Payabo's assistant: “add my mum as a recipient”) creates or edits a recipient, the operation is a mutating tool and must route through the Spec 032 fail-closed gate like any other. Suggested classification:

Recipient toolTierRationale
Read a recipient / list recipientsRead onlyNo state change; executes directly.
Create / update a recipient (incl. add a rail)MediumAn everyday domain write that introduces a payable destination — reversible, but it shapes where money can later go. Persists a ToolApprovalRequest; runs in-band once after a server-validated confirm.
Remove a recipientLow–MediumReversible soft-removal; Low if treated as personal-state, Medium if the product wants an explicit confirm.
A recipient never moves money Creating a recipient is not a High action — no funds move, no ledger posts, no partner is called. Money movement happens only when an Order later references the recipient and a Payment executes; those are the High, proposal-marshalled actions. The recipient surface stays on the address-book side of the “agents propose, systems execute” line.

11. Data model & migrations

The headline is that almost nothing new is stored. The five entities all exist with shipped EF configurations and the Ank-prefixed tables (ExternalPayoutAccount → AnkExternalPayoutAccounts, indexed on (TenantId, BeneficiaryPartyId) and (TenantId, ConnectorId)). Likely changes are small and additive:

  • Soft-delete on the rail. If ExternalPayoutAccount does not already carry the base soft-delete column from AuditableEntity / AonikDbContextBase, removal relies on it; confirm before adding anything.
  • Party photo read contract. The §8 seam is a contract change (a richer PartyProfileResponse or a SetPartyPhotoAsync), not necessarily a schema change — the photo columns already exist on PersonProfile.
  • List index. Search-by-name + paging may want an index supporting the name filter; measure against Party.DisplayName before adding.
Migrations are tool-generated, single-stream — no exceptions If any schema change is needed, it goes through one dotnet ef migrations add <Name> --project src/Aonik.Infrastructure --startup-project src/Aonik.Api against AonikDbContext only, with the entity configuration in its module's Persistence/Configurations/ folder. No hand-written migration files, no edits to module-scoped contexts' migration streams (per CLAUDE.md). The expectation for this spec is zero or one tiny migration — most of the work is service + endpoint + contract, not schema.

13. Acceptance criteria

  • dotnet build Aonik.sln and dotnet test Aonik.sln succeed; any schema change is one tool-generated migration that applies cleanly from the single stream.
  • No new Recipient table. A recipient remains Party + Recipient edge + Beneficiary role + ExternalPayoutAccount; the service composes the existing seams and does not reimplement the graph stitching.
  • Create reuses an existing party when a party id is supplied and creates one otherwise, idempotently ensuring the edge + role (verified against the shipped SaveBeneficiaryAsync behaviour).
  • Get-one & list return identity + relationship + photo URLs + saved rails, scoped to the owning customer; list supports search, page, pageSize with a stable sort; a recipient party id alone never reads another customer's recipient.
  • Update edits display/relationship/notes and/or a named rail without duplicating the edge.
  • Soft-remove deactivates the relationship edge and soft-deletes its rails; a seeded historical order referencing that recipient party is provably unchanged afterwards.
  • Photo upload produces original + 3 thumbnails via IProfilePhotoStore onto the recipient's PersonProfile, returns the URLs, and rejects missing/empty/invalid images with 422.
  • Sensitive data. No raw account number / MSISDN / wallet id is persisted or returned — only the masked identifier + the connector token (Spec 031).
  • Tenant isolation. All recipient reads/writes filter by tenant; cross-tenant access is impossible through the surface.
  • Agent safety. If exposed as an agent tool, create/update/remove are classified (Medium/Low) and routed through the Spec 032 gate; none is High — the recipient surface moves no money.

14. Open decisions

Resolved Shape. Recipient = party graph, not a new table — ratifying the code as built. Storage. Rails stay in Finance (ExternalPayoutAccount), identity + photo in Platform (Party / PersonProfile). Photos. Reuse IProfilePhotoStore; a recipient photo is a party photo. Deletion. Soft, edge-level, order-preserving.
Open Route convergence. Alias, redirect, or retain /payments/payout-beneficiaries once /recipients lands? An alias is least disruptive for current Admin UI / Payabo callers; a redirect is cleaner long-term but needs a caller sweep.
Open Party-photo seam shape. Extend the read to a PartyProfileResponse carrying photo URLs, or add explicit Set/GetPartyPhotoAsync to IPartyService? The read-extension is lighter for the recipient projection; the explicit methods are clearer for the write. Pick one before §8 is built (§11/Task 2).
Open Multiple rails per recipient in the product UI. The model already supports N rails per recipient (one party, several ExternalPayoutAccount rows). Whether the Payabo MVP exposes “a recipient with several payout methods” or collapses to one-rail-per-recipient for launch is a product call that shapes RecipientResponse.Rails cardinality in the UI (not the model).
Open Business recipients & photos. A Business recipient has a BusinessProfile, which has no photo columns. Decide whether business recipients show a logo (new field / different store) or fall back to an initial/monogram in the UI.
