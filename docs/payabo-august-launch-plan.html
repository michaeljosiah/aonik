1.Executive Summary

The AONIK platform is a great deal more built than it is launchable. The honest verdict, after walking the code rather than the docs, is this: you have spent serious craft on a personal-finance substrate that works (accounts, transactions, Plaid linking, household, dashboard, identity, document storage, notifications). You have almost none of the remittance plumbing that the Payabo wedge actually needs — no real cross-border partner, no recipient/NUBAN model, no remittance order type, and a payment gateway that is a Stripe simulation in name only.

That asymmetry is the central fact of the next ninety days. The plan below treats it as such.

MVP — what we will actually ship

A single mobile app that lets a Nigerian professional in London (a) send money to a known family beneficiary in Nigeria, (b) pay one of four high-frequency Nigerian bills on a family member's behalf, (c) see a clean record of "money I have sent home this month" and "bills I have promised to cover," and (d) reach a human if anything breaks. Manual KYC. One corridor. One partner. Fifty hand-picked users. No goals, no voice, no Compass, no web app.

Top three launch risks

  1. Regulatory authority for moving money — Payabo cannot operate as a principal under FCA in this timeframe. The only credible route is to ride on a regulated partner's permission (agent model or PSP-bears-liability). This decision needs to be made and contracted this week; everything else cascades from it.
  2. No real remittance partner is integrated. The Finance module's OrderType enum literally contains BillPayment, BankTransfer, CashCollection — no CrossBorderRemittance. There is one paper payment gateway (StripeSimulatedPaymentProviderGateway) and four entirely fictional partners in seed data. Pick one real counterparty (recommendation: Flutterwave for Africa-corridor coverage and biller pay in a single integration) and build the connector now.
  3. Single-founder bus factor on a regulated product. AI agents can hold open the typing speed of three engineers; they cannot sit on the legal contract with the FCA-authorised partner, cannot front a beta-user call, and cannot make the judgement call when a wire goes wrong. The work that must be done by you needs to be named explicitly and ring-fenced against the temptation to keep coding.

Honest verdict on August readiness

August 2026 is achievable only if the plan below is followed with discipline: one corridor, one partner, no platform work, manual compliance for the first fifty users, and a ruthless freeze on Compass / voice / household-v2 / customer-insight-AI. If any one of those four commitments slips — particularly the partner contract — September becomes the realistic ceiling, and that is still a win. Targeting July or earlier is not honest. Targeting August "fully self-licensed" is fantasy. Targeting "late-August soft launch via a regulated partner to fifty named users" is the credible answer.

The one-sentence verdict
Thirty-four commits landed in fourteen days and the engineering was genuinely good — but it flowed almost entirely into platform and agent depth, not the remittance wedge, and the mobile app, the entire intended launch surface, received zero commits. The plan's own Tier-3 risk — "sunk-cost gravity from AONIK platform breadth" — is precisely the thing that happened in Sprint 1.

2A.1 What genuinely moved toward the wedge

  • The test projects now exist, with the right first test. tests/Aonik.Finance.Tests and tests/Aonik.PersonalFinance.Tests were stood up (commit 8c423a51), and the ledger double-entry invariant is locked down both in-memory (LedgerInvariantTests) and against real SQL Server via Testcontainers (LedgerInvariantSqlServerTests). A shared Aonik.TestSupport fixtures library and a transactional outbox also landed. This was Sprint 1's first backend task and it is done. Caveat: the PersonalFinance project is a single placeholder smoke test — the harness exists, real coverage does not yet.
  • OrderType gained a Remittance value. The enum is no longer the three values the plan grepped; it is now BillPayment, BankTransfer, CashCollection, Payout, Collection, AirtimeTopup, Remittance (OrderType.cs). It is explicitly "service-intent only" — the actual rail lives on execution entities — so this is a name, not a working flow, but the literal claim "no CrossBorderRemittance value" is now out of date.
  • A partner-integration abstraction landed (Spec 031). The IPartnerConnector shape the plan asked Sprint 1 to "sketch" was fully built: IPartnerPayoutConnector / IPartnerCollectionConnector / IPartnerBillPaymentConnector, an IPartnerConnectorResolver, webhook translation, and a full schema (Connector, ConnectorCapability, ConnectorBillerMapping, PartnerWebhookEvent, PartnerBillPayment, Transmission tables). The payout port even includes QuotePayoutAsync and ResolveAccountAsync (name-enquiry). But the only concrete implementation is SimulatedPartnerConnector — still zero real vendor adapters, and nothing actually invokes the resolve→initiate→record→settle path end-to-end. The seam is real; the money still cannot move.
  • Payout-beneficiary storage exists (commit 8947bf66). ExternalPayoutAccount + PayoutBeneficiaryService + save/list endpoints + a validator now persist a destination and stitch the customer→recipient party graph. This is adjacent to the Spec 008 recipient model but is not it: no NUBAN validation, no name-enquiry wired (IsVerified is hardcoded false), and Spec 008's /recipients CRUD remains unimplemented.
  • Financial-correctness and security hardening. A system-initiated idempotent LedgerPostingService, idempotency-key normalisation and race handling on order/payment creation, and — most importantly for a regulated product — a fail-closed tenant filter (commit 8559383c): the platform previously returned all tenants' rows on any query that ran without a resolved tenant. That was a live cross-tenant data leak, and it is now closed. Two CVE-flagged packages (DataProtection, Cryptography.Xml) were also patched.

2A.2 The plan's central blocker was solved — under a different number

Correction to §2.6 and to "Recommended Cuts"
The plan's repeated claim — "the server-side mutating-tool approval wrapper (spec 023's ApprovalRequiredAIFunction) does not exist in code" — is no longer true. It was built under Spec 032 (which supersedes the never-built Spec 023). IToolApprovalGate + ApprovalGatedAIFunction now wrap every classified mutating tool and fail closed: an unclassified mutating-looking tool throws ToolNotClassifiedException at agent-build time. Three tiers are enforced — low runs in-band with an audit record, medium persists a durable args-hash-bound ToolApprovalRequest and runs once only after a human approves via POST /ai/tool-approvals/{id}/decide, high (the four money tools) is never run in-band and is marshalled into a Spec 030 Proposal. It is wired for the Finance agent (FinanceToolApprovalManifest) and the approval cards now surface in the AG-UI chat stream (commit 6e59826f). Still genuinely pending: voice parity — the voice agent stays safe the old way, by filtering mutating tools out entirely rather than gating them.

2A.3 Scope the plan said to freeze — built anyway

This is the part to sit with. The "do not do" lanes named Compass, voice, household-v2, the web app, and "any agent skill." Three of the four largest features merged since 24 May are platform/agent breadth, and were not in the plan at all:

  • Spec 033 — tenant-managed agent extensibility (commit faf7d1ca, ~27,000 insertions across 56 files, plus an admin-UI hub and an Agent Detail redesign). Lets a tenant admin extend agents via uploaded MAF skills, remote MCP servers, and HTTP/OpenAPI tools. Sophisticated, correctly routed through the Spec 032 gate — and exactly the kind of B2B platform surface the plan argued has "zero commercial value in 2026" before Payabo has users. This is the current branch.
  • Spec 035 — Documents-module extraction + RAG (ADR-009). The document capability was lifted out of Platform into a new Aonik.Documents module with a Qdrant vector index, party-scoped retrieval isolation (which did close a real B2C cross-customer leak), and a right-to-erasure path. Genuinely good engineering; not on the critical path to moving a pound.
  • Spec 034 — WorkItem scheduling (ADR-008). A general-purpose data-defined task/reminder primitive with a Quartz heartbeat dispatcher, preserving human-in-the-loop. Powers AI-chat reminders.
  • Spec 024 Voice Phase C (the Active-Settings cutover the plan listed as "not done") shipped. MAF / MCP / Microsoft.Extensions.AI were upgraded to GA, and two standalone MCP servers (Aonik.Finance.Mcp, Aonik.Platform.Mcp) now exist.

Defensible exception: Spec 032 (the approval gate) and the fail-closed tenant filter are foundational security work for any money product — building them is not drift. The rest is.

2A.5 What this means for the August date

The honest read is unchanged in shape but tighter in time. The remittance critical path — real partner connector, NUBAN name-enquiry, transmission processor that actually executes, live FX, four real billers, a send-money endpoint, and a mobile send-money flow — has barely moved; the abstraction layer is a head-start on the connector and nothing more. Meanwhile two of the seven sprints' worth of engineering capacity went to off-wedge platform work, and the launch surface (mobile) is untouched.

August is still reachable, but only by treating the next sprint as a hard reset onto the wedge. If the partner contract is not signed and mobile send-money is not under construction by the end of Sprint 2 (21 Jun), the credible target moves to September. The compliance holes flagged in May — ComplianceService.ScreenPartyAsync still returns "Passed" for every party, and IdvStatus still has no verifier — remain exactly as open as they were, and still must close before live money moves.

The course-correction, in one move
Stop adding platform breadth. The codebase does not need another module; it needs the mobile send-money flow and one real partner connector. For the next two sprints, the only new backend code should be the concrete partner connector behind the Spec 031 seam and the transmission processor that drives it — and the only new product code should be in apps/payabo_mobile. Treat Spec 033/034/035-style work as frozen, not paused.

3.MVP Definition

3.1 The user

Call her Adaeze. She is 34, lives in Stratford, works as a senior analyst in financial services or healthcare, sends £400–£900 home most months — split between her mother's housekeeping, her younger sister's tuition top-up, and the family's DSTV and electricity bill. She currently does this through some combination of Wise, Lemfi, WhatsApp, calling her brother to "go pay PHCN," and remembering. She is high-trust, busy, slightly embarrassed at the patchwork, and intensely loyal to whoever finally makes it feel calm.

3.2 Core jobs-to-be-done

  1. "Send Mum her money for the month."
  2. "Pay the family's PHCN / DSTV / GoTV / MTN airtime — directly, from here, without calling anyone."
  3. "Remind me what I have committed to cover this month and whether I have done it."
  4. "Show me, on one screen, what I have sent home this year and what's coming up."
  5. "If something goes wrong with my transfer, get a real person to fix it."

4.Gap Analysis

4.1 Engineering gaps

Backend

▲ 6 Jun: partially advanced — the Remittance enum value, the Spec 031 connector abstraction, payout-beneficiary storage, and the test projects all landed. Still entirely open: a real connector, NUBAN name-enquiry, the transmission processor, the live FX feed, the four real billers, and the send-money endpoint.

  • Remittance order type. Add CrossBorderRemittance to OrderType in src/Aonik.Finance/Entities/Orders/OrderType.cs. Build a RemittanceOrderService sibling to OrderService. Wire idempotency, party-role routing (payer/beneficiary), FX-quote attachment.
  • Recipient model. Implement spec 008: Recipient entity (party + bank-account + relationship), validation, NUBAN account-resolution via the chosen partner's name-enquiry endpoint.
  • One real partner connector. Replace StripeSimulatedPaymentProviderGateway's role with a real IPartnerConnector implementation against Flutterwave (or alternative — see §7.2). Cover: payout creation, status polling, webhook ingestion, idempotent retry.
  • Transmission processor. Quartz job that picks pending Transmission rows, calls the partner connector, advances state, retries on failure.

4.2 Compliance & regulatory gaps

This is the section where polite hedging gets people fined.

  • Permission to handle client money. Three doors. (a) Become an FCA Small Payment Institution or Electronic Money Institution — 9–18 months and ~£20k+ capital. Off the table for August. (b) Become an FCA Registered Agent of an existing API/EMI — 4–8 weeks if the principal will sponsor. (c) White-label / refer flows to a regulated PSP who holds the regulatory relationship with the user directly — fastest, lightest, lowest brand control. The plan assumes (c) via a single partner, with (b) as a parallel track to move to before scale.
  • KYC / IDV. No vendor is integrated. Onfido or Veriff is the right answer pre-scale; for the first 50, accept manual review. Document everything you do so the partner accepts your KYC for their AML obligations (most partners will not — they will run their own IDV on top regardless).
  • Sanctions / PEP screening. ComplianceService returns "Passed" for everyone. For 50 users you can screen against the UK OFSI consolidated list manually (it is a downloadable CSV). Get a contract with ComplyAdvantage or Refinitiv in motion now — even if it lands post-launch, you cannot scale past 200 users without it.
  • Transaction monitoring. No rule engine exists. For 50 users, monitor manually with a daily admin dashboard query. Build a basic rules engine in v1.1.
  • ICO registration / GDPR. Register with the ICO (~£40, online). Publish privacy policy. Implement the data-deletion request flow (you already have hard-delete from spec 026).
  • Terms of Service / fee disclosure. Required before any user transacts. Use a fintech-aware UK lawyer for the first draft; do not do this with ChatGPT.
  • Consumer Duty (FCA). Even via a partner, you are likely a distributor for FCA Consumer Duty purposes. Document your fair-value assessment for the first cohort.
  • Data residency / cross-border data. UK customer data on Azure UK regions. Transfer to Nigerian partner is a controlled transfer — covered under your partner DPA.

7.Risks and Mitigations

Tier 1
Date-killer

Partner contract slips or falls through

If no partner has signed by end of Sprint 2, August launch is at serious risk. Every dependent piece of work assumes a known counterparty.

Mitigation: Have two partners in conversation from S1, not one. Be willing to take worse commercial terms for faster contract speed. Have the lawyer pre-drafted on a "standard distributor agreement" template so signing is a redline exercise, not a build-from-scratch.

Tier 1
Date-killer

Regulatory misclassification

You believe you are operating as a partner's distributor; FCA later disagrees and you are operating as an unauthorised payment institution. This is the kind of error that ends companies.

Mitigation: Get the legal opinion in writing in Sprint 1. Have the partner explicitly confirm in the contract that they are the regulated entity and the customer relationship for payment-services purposes sits with them. Do not move a single live pound until that letter is in your inbox.

Tier 1
Date-killer

Single-founder bus factor on regulated activity

Anyone-fall-ill-or-overwhelmed scenario, with no second party who knows the partner contract, the compliance procedure, or where the keys are kept.

Mitigation: Document everything as you go in /docs/operations/. Runbooks for every recurring process. A trusted second person (lawyer, partner-side contact, or co-founder-of-record) who has the contract, the password vault recovery, and a one-page "if Michael goes silent for 72 hours, here's what to do." This is not pessimism; it is the bare minimum for handling other people's money.

Tier 2
Quality-killer

Manual KYC backlog

If KYC clearance takes > 24h, sign-up conversion drops sharply and users abandon. At 50 users you can handle it; the moment you grow, this breaks.

Mitigation: Strict 24h SLA, founder + VA on it. Start the Onfido / Veriff contract conversation in S3 so you are ready to flip the switch in v1.1. Have a written "what counts as an acceptable ID" matrix so the VA does not need to escalate every borderline case.

Tier 2
Quality-killer

FX rate gap between displayed and settled

User sees one NGN amount on the confirm screen and a different amount lands. Single biggest trust-killer in remittance.

Mitigation: Lock partner-provided rate per quote for ≥ 5 minutes. Display the locked NGN amount, not a derived recalculation. Reconcile daily. If a discrepancy of > 0.1% appears, automated alert + manual review.

Tier 2
Quality-killer

Plaid UK coverage gaps

Some UK challenger banks (Monzo, Starling, Revolut UK) have non-uniform Plaid coverage. A user who can't link their primary account loses the spending-side product story.

Mitigation: Plaid is optional in v1. The send-money flow funds via partner card / open-banking pay-in, not via Plaid balance. Plaid linking is an enrichment layer for the "commitments detected" feature, gracefully absent if not linked.

Tier 2
Quality-killer

App Store rejection

iOS App Review for fintech apps can take 2 weeks and reject for unclear KYC flow, missing privacy disclosures, or in-app finance promises that smell like investment advice.

Mitigation: Submit by S4 not S6. Be very precise in the listing about what the app does and who is the regulated entity behind it. Have the privacy policy URL working. Avoid any "save money" / "grow your money" / "AI advisor" language.

Tier 2
Quality-killer

Nigerian biller API instability

NG biller APIs (especially PHCN / discos) have variable uptime. A user pays £20 of PHCN credit and the meter doesn't recharge for 3 hours. They blame Payabo, not Eko Disco.

Mitigation: Status-aware UI ("usually within 5 minutes; can take up to 30 if the disco is slow"). Customer service script for the inevitable angry WhatsApp. Choose your partner partly on biller-uptime track record — ask for SLA data in S1.

Tier 3
Survivable

AI agent over-confident on engineering work

Agents will happily write a migration by hand even though CLAUDE.md forbids it. They will happily implement a "household v2 phase 2 — should be quick." Drift accumulates silently.

Mitigation: Founder code-reviews every PR. Treat the "do not do" list in each sprint as enforceable — if it shipped, push back hard. Use the existing review skills (/review, /ultrareview, /security-review) on every PR that touches money or auth.

▲ 6 Jun — partly fired. Migrations stayed tool-generated (good), but ~27k LOC of off-plan agent breadth (Spec 033) merged. The discipline gap is not hand-written SQL — it is letting well-built, well-reviewed, off-wedge features in the door. The "do not do" lane needs to gate scope, not just quality.

Tier 3
Survivable

Beta users are too charitable

You recruited from your own network. They will smile and say "lovely" rather than tell you the truth. You won't see real product problems until users 51+.

Mitigation: Structure user conversations around what they did, not what they think (see The Mom Test, Rob Fitzpatrick — required reading). Track what they actually use, not what they say they will. Be explicit with them: "your job is to find the embarrassing problems."

Tier 3
Survivable

Sunk-cost gravity from AONIK platform breadth

The platform has Compass-shaped, voice-shaped, household-shaped, web-shaped, agent-shaped, MCP-shaped things in it. The temptation to "just turn this one on" will be constant.

Mitigation: The "Recommended Cuts" section below is the answer. Re-read it weekly. The codebase that ships in August will be smaller than the codebase that exists today, on purpose.
